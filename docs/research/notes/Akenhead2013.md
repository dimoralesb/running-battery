# Akenhead2013: Acceleration and deceleration output declines during match play
Citation: Akenhead R, Hayes PR, Thompson KG, French D. Diminutions of acceleration and deceleration output during professional football match play. J Sci Med Sport. 2013;16(6):556-561. doi:10.1016/j.jsams.2012.12.005
Type: match study (small sample)
Context: **English Premier Reserve League** (reserve / development level, not first-team Premier League), 2010/11, 18 matches, 105 x 68 m natural grass, all teams 4-4-2. 36 players from two clubs (one is Newcastle United, the funder), **age 19.3 ± 0.5 y**, some with U21 and full international caps. CD 8, WD 8, CM 8, WM 6, F 6 (p.557). **GPS**: Catapult MinimaxX, non-differential, **10 Hz**, Logan Plus v4.5 software (p.557). 13 ± 1 satellites, HDOP 0.8 ± 0.1 (p.559). Only full-match players in the same position, and first halves with ≤ 2 added minutes. Only the first 45 min of each half were analysed (stoppage time removed).
Evidence strength: low-medium. Repeated measures with effect sizes and CIs, but n = 36, reserve level, early 10 Hz GPS (validity for high accelerations is uncertain), not split by position, and internal inconsistencies in the reported totals (below).

## Definitions and thresholds
Journal pages (PDF page = journal page - 555).
**Acceleration and deceleration are measured as DISTANCE (m) covered while accelerating within a band, not as counts of efforts.**
| Variable | Band |
|---|---|
| TACC (total acceleration) | > 1 m/s² |
| LACC (low) | 1-2 m/s² |
| MACC (moderate) | 2-3 m/s² |
| HACC (high) | > 3 m/s² |
| TDEC (total deceleration) | < -1 m/s² |
| LDEC | -1 to -2 m/s² |
| MDEC | -2 to -3 m/s² |
| HDEC | < -3 m/s² |
| HSR | ≥ 5.8 m/s (20.9 km/h) (Table 1 footnote; methods text writes "> 5.8 m s⁻²", a typo) |
| SPD (sprint) | ≥ 6.7 m/s (24.1 km/h) (Table 1); methods text says > 6.78 (24.4 km/h) |
(p.556-557.) No minimum effort duration (dwell time) is stated. The bands were "arbitrary", based on unpublished data (p.557).
Periods: P1 0-15, P2 16-30, P3 31-45, P4 46-60, P5 61-75, P6 76-90 min. Transient analysis: PEAK = the 5-min block (of 18 fixed blocks) with the most HACC (or HDEC); 5PRE = the block before; 5POST = the block after; 10POST = the block after that. All are expressed as % of the mean 5-min value of that half (x̄45) (p.557).

Post hoc details (p.557-559): TACC P1 > P3, P5, P6 (CI 6.0-49.6 m, ES 0.67-1.51); P2 and P4 > P6 (ES 0.60-0.94). HDEC P1 > P6 (p = 0.002, CI 1.4-10.8 m, ES 0.92). P4 (first 15 min after half-time) recovers to about the P2 level, so half-time gives partial recovery.
Ball-in-play time did not change significantly across periods (largest drop 6%, P1 to P6, p = 1.0) (p.559). So the decline is not explained by less ball-in-play time.

Recovery to the mean takes about 5 min (10POST back at ~100%) (p.559-560).

Share of total distance (p.559): 18% of TD at > 1 m/s² or < -1 m/s²; 7.5% at 1-2, 4.3% at 2-3, 3.3% at > 3 m/s² (accel + decel combined).
Reliability (unpublished, p.560): match-to-match CV of HACC/HDEC 12-25%, vs HSR 25-45% and sprint 30-47.5%.

## Equations
Transient values (p.557; checked against PDF):
$$ X_{\%} = 100 \cdot \frac{X_{5\,\mathrm{min}}}{\bar{x}_{45}} $$
- $X_{5\,\mathrm{min}}$: HACC or HDEC distance (m) in the 5-min block (5PRE, PEAK, 5POST or 10POST)
- $\bar{x}_{45}$: mean 5-min HACC or HDEC distance (m) over the 9 blocks of that half

## Limitations
- **Internal inconsistency in Table 1**: TACC (1022 m) = LACC + MACC + HACC would require LACC ≈ 602 m, not 424 m; TDEC (899) would require LDEC ≈ 527 m, not 365 m. Fig. 1 period values add up to ≈ 602 m (LACC) and ≈ 527 m (LDEC), which agree with TACC/TDEC. The "18% of total distance" statement also matches TACC + TDEC, while "7.5% at 1-2 m/s²" matches the Table 1 LACC/LDEC. **So either Table 1 LACC/LDEC are wrong, or the low band is defined differently.** Use TACC/TDEC and the Fig. 1 period values; treat Table 1 LACC/LDEC as doubtful.
- Reserve league, mean age 19 y, two clubs, 4-4-2 only. Not first-team EPL.
- 10 Hz non-differential GPS (2010-era MinimaxX). Acceleration values depend heavily on the device, filtering and dwell time. They are **not comparable with optical tracking** (TRACAB/Second Spectrum). Allen2024 even states optical systems cannot measure accelerations accurately.
- Distance in band, not number of efforts. Effort counts (the more common metric now) are not reported.
- No positional split (sample too small, p.560).
- Fixed 5-min blocks; the post-peak drop is partly regression to the mean (no control for this).
- Only the first 45 min of each half; stoppage time excluded.
- No context control (score, substitutions of opponents, tactics).

## Use in Running Battery
- Simulator: the hidden fatigue should reduce **acceleration/deceleration volume** by ~15-21% from the first to the last 15 min, with partial recovery after half-time (P4 ≈ P2), while HSR/sprint distance per half can stay flat. A post-peak dip in high accelerations of ~10% for ~5 min, recovering by 10 min. Note this is volume (fewer metres accelerating), not proof of lower maximal acceleration capacity.
- Fatigue estimator: rolling high-acceleration/deceleration distance vs the player's own half-mean is a candidate signal, with ~10% transient dips after peaks. Because the numbers come from GPS, our 10 Hz synthetic positions need their own calibration; do not reuse these absolute metres directly. Acceleration from position data requires smoothing; the band results depend on the filter.
- Agents / claims: allowed: "In a reserve-league GPS study, distance covered while accelerating/decelerating fell ~15-21% from the first to the last 15 min." Not allowed: "players lose acceleration capacity" (they measured output volume, not capacity) or "high accelerations decline significantly" (HACC main effect was n.s., p = 0.08; HDEC was significant only P1 vs P6).
- Overlay / UI: none directly.

## Ideas or questions it raised
- READING_LIST says "Accelerations decline over the match. Decline per 15-min period." Correct in direction, but it should say: reserve league, GPS 10 Hz, distance-based bands, HACC decline not significant, and HSR/sprint did NOT decline between halves.
- PLAN.md (section 2, hidden physiology) cites Akenhead2013 for "low energy lowers top speed and acceleration". The paper shows lower acceleration **volume**; it did not measure top speed or maximal acceleration. Rephrase to "players perform less acceleration and deceleration work late in the match".
- The Table 1 vs Fig. 1 inconsistency should be noted if anyone uses the per-band totals.
- The finding that HSR/sprint stayed flat while accelerations fell suggests a fatigue estimator focused only on speed thresholds could miss fatigue. Adding an acceleration-based channel (e.g. Morin's acceleration-speed profile or metabolic power) could help.
