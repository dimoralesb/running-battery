# SilvaJR2018: Acute and residual match-related fatigue (systematic review and meta-analysis)
Citation: Silva JR, Rumpf MC, Hertzog M, Castagna C, Farooq A, Girard O, Hader K (2018). Acute and residual soccer match-related fatigue: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Sports Medicine 48(3):539–583. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-017-0798-8
Type: review (systematic review + random-effects meta-analysis for post-match protocol comparison)
Context: 77 studies, 1105 players (male and female, inclusion ≥18 y, although Rampinini 2008 juniors appear in Table 1 as 18 ± 1 y), 1196 effect sizes; search to March 2016 (PubMed, SportDiscus). Protocols: real 11 vs 11 matches (40 studies, 635 players; official and friendly), on-field simulations (27 studies, 346 players: LIST, SAFT90, SMS, CST, SGM) and treadmill simulations (12 studies, 147 players) (p. 544). Levels: amateur, NCAA, semi-pro, professional, elite (Table 1). No tracking data: outcomes are tests done before/after (sprint gates, jumps, dynamometers, LSPT, blood, questionnaires).
Evidence strength: high for the existence and time course of post-match neuromuscular fatigue (many studies). Medium-low for half-time values (few ES per outcome, e.g. 2 ES for linear sprint) and for technical outcomes (6 studies, 92 players). Effect sizes pool test results, not in-match running.

## Definitions and thresholds
- Time points: baseline (pre), half-time (HT), Post (match end), G+24 h, G+48 h, G+72 h. Measurements were rounded to the nearest 24 h (e.g. 18 h counted as 24 h) (p. 541).
- ES magnitude (p. 544): trivial <0.2, small 0.2–0.6, moderate 0.6–1.2, large 1.2–2.0, very large >2.0.
- Sign convention: ES and % change are (post − pre). For sprint TIME, a positive ES means slower (worse). For jump height, strength, power, a negative ES means worse.
- "Moderators" coded: training status (low <4 h/week, moderate 5–7 h, high >8 h), surface (artificial turf / synthetic / grass), temperature (cold ≤15 °C, temperate 17–25 °C, hot ≥27 °C), protocol type (11 vs 11 / on-field / treadmill) (p. 542). Only protocol type was actually analysed (Figs 3–4). The others were not tested (Limitations, p. 576–577).
- Technical outcomes: LSPT (penalties, time, total) and LSST (Loughborough Soccer Shooting Test: shot speed, shooting score) (p. 542).

In real 11 vs 11 matches, CIs for 10 m sprint, CMJ and all LSPT scores cross zero. Real matches give larger muscle damage (CK), inflammation and soreness than simulations; neuromuscular losses are similar (p. 576).

Other headline numbers (abstract, p. 539): at HT, lower-limb muscle function ES −0.5 to −0.7; physical performance "e.g. linear sprint" ES −0.3 to −1.0; insulin ES −0.9; leukocytes ES 1.9. Still impaired at G+72 h: hamstring force (ES −0.7), physical performance (2–4%, ES 0.3–0.5), CK (ES 0.4), well-being (ES 0.2–0.4), DOMS (ES 0.6–1.3).

Perceptual (Table 6): Hooper fatigue Post +46.7% ES 1.1; 24 h +37.0% ES 0.9; 48 h +15.8% ES 0.4; 72 h +11.3% ES 0.3. DOMS general 24 h ES 1.3, 72 h ES 0.6.

## Equations     (LaTeX, with variable definitions and units; mark "checked against PDF")
Checked against PDF (p. 543, Eqs 1–5, viewed as image).
$$\Delta\% = \frac{M_{post} - M_{pre}}{M_{pre}} \times 100$$
$$ES = g = \frac{M_{post} - M_{pre}}{SD_{pooled}}, \qquad SD_{pooled} = \sqrt{\frac{(n-1)\,SD_{pre}^2 + (n-1)\,SD_{post}^2}{2n-2}}$$
$$CF = 1 - \frac{3}{4\,df - 1}, \quad df = n - 1, \qquad ES_c = g \times CF$$
$M_{pre}$, $M_{post}$: group mean of the test (units of the test: s, cm, N·m…) at baseline and at the later time point; $SD_{pre}$, $SD_{post}$: their SDs; $n$: players in the study; $CF$: Hedges small-sample correction (dimensionless); $ES_c$: corrected effect size (dimensionless).

## Limitations
- Half-time values rest on very few ES (linear sprint: 2 ES, 34 observations; CMJ: 3 ES). Treat them as rough.
- The ES uses the pooled SD of pre and post, not the SD of the change. This is between-subject scaling; it does not reflect within-player reliability.
- Pools 11 vs 11 matches with lab simulations for Tables 4–6. Simulations have no tactics, no opponents and often no ball.
- Moderators (surface, heat, training status, sex) were coded but not analysed. Only protocol type was compared.
- Tests are done off the pitch (gates, force plates). They measure capacity, not what players chose to run in the match. A 3% slower 10 m sprint test is not the same as 3% lower in-match top speed.
- Contradiction inside the paper: text says sprint "recovered at G+72 h", but 6-s sprint peak speed is −4.8% (ES −0.9) at 72 h (1 ES, 10 players).
- Text p. 569 says LSPT data come from "four studies, 82 players" but cites six references.
- Inclusion said ≥18 y, yet 17–18-year-old juniors (Rampinini 2008) were included.

## Use in Running Battery
- Simulator: hidden "capacity" for sprint should fall about 2–4% by match end (test-based sprint time +3.5% Post, ES 0.6) and about 3% already at half-time if we follow HT data (weak). Use a smaller drop for real-match realism (11 vs 11 10 m sprint ES 0.38, CI crosses zero). Recovery for multi-match simulation: sprint back by 72 h; jump and hamstring strength not fully back at 72 h.
- Fatigue estimator: supports using drop in max speed / acceleration capacity as a fatigue signal, but in-match running is self-paced, so test-based ES are an upper bound for what we will see in tracking. For multi-match mode (if any), carry a residual-fatigue term of ~1–2% sprint capacity at 48 h.
- Agents / claims: allowed: "players typically lose a few percent of sprint speed by the end of a match, and it can take 2–3 days to recover" (supported, test data). Allowed with caution: "already at half-time some sprint capacity is lost" (few studies). Not allowed: "fatigue ruins passing in matches" (11 vs 11 LSPT CIs cross zero; overall technical effect trivial to small).
- Overlay / UI: a "recovery days" chip for between-match context (sprint ~48–72 h, jump/hamstrings >72 h) could be a nice analyst detail.

## Ideas or questions it raised
- READING_LIST claim check: "77 studies" correct. "At half-time, sprint performance already drops (ES −0.3 to −1.0)": imprecise. The −0.3 to −1.0 range in the abstract covers several half-time physical tests. Linear sprint time at HT is +2.9%, ES 1.0 (from only 2 ES, 34 observations); RSA mean time is ES 0.3; 6-s sprint mean speed is trivial (ES 0.1); CMJ is trivial (ES −0.1). Better wording: "At half-time, 10-m sprint time is ~3% slower (ES ~1.0, 2 effect sizes only); post-match sprint time is 3.5% slower (ES 0.6, 18 ES); jump and strength losses persist beyond 72 h."
- Accuracy (LSPT penalties) is the only technical part that moves: +46% post-match (ES 0.5) but with huge range (3–157%) and mostly from simulations. Execution time hardly changes (ES 0.1). Matches Rampinini 2008.
- Real matches produce smaller test-based neuromuscular drops than simulations for CMJ and sprint (Fig 4). This supports self-pacing: in matches players protect themselves. Our simulator's agents should pace too.
