# Allen2025: A decade of Premier League running load and fixture congestion, 2015/16 to 2024/25
Citation: Allen T, Taberner M, Zhilkin M, Green M. Evolving running load demands and fixture congestion in the English Premier League: a decade of insights from 2015/2016 to 2024/2025. Br J Sports Med 2025;59:1643–1654. doi:10.1136/bjsports-2025-110430. Open access (CC BY-NC 4.0). Published version, 12 PDF pages (journal pages 1643–1654).
Type: match study (league-wide, longitudinal, team level)
Context: English Premier League, all clubs, 10 seasons 2015/16 to 2024/25. 34 teams, 1,912 players, 3,750 games; 50 without tracking and 342 not finishing 11 v 11 removed, leaving **3,408 games** (p.1644). Optical tracking at 25 Hz: TRACAB Gen4 (2015/16–2018/19), then Second Spectrum ("2S", 2019/20–2024/25). TRACAB values were converted to 2S with calibration equations (Fig. 1). Minutes and congestion from Wyscout. **Team-level only: no positions, no individual players.** Goalkeepers are included in team totals (p.1644).
Evidence strength: **high** for current EPL team totals (whole league, 10 seasons, one current provider for the last 6). **Not usable for per-position values.** The system switch in 2019/20 may inflate part of the trend in high-speed zones, which the authors themselves say (p.1652, limitations).

## Definitions and thresholds
| Metric | Definition (p.1644) |
|---|---|
| TD | total distance (m), team total |
| HID | high-intensity distance, > 5.5 m/s (19.8 km/h), **includes** sprint (HID = HSR + SprD; checked: 2024/25 7,285 + 2,326 = 9,611) |
| HSR | 5.5–7 m/s (19.8–25.2 km/h) |
| SprD | > 7 m/s (25.2 km/h) |
| Dwell time | 0.5 s for HSR, 1 s for SprD, both systems |
| "/min" | **per player-slot per minute, including the GK.** Not stated in the paper; back-calculated: 113,891 m / (11 × 99.5 min) = 104.06 m/min vs reported 104.08 (2024/25); 2015/16: 111,585 / (11 × 96.3) = 105.3 vs 105.22 |
| Congested match | 3rd game within 8 days, plus any game ≤ 3 days after a congested one (p.1644) |
| Effect size | Cohen's d: trivial < 0.2, small 0.2–0.6, moderate 0.6–1.2, large 1.2–2.0 (p.1645) |

**Change 2015/16 → 2024/25** (Fig. 2, p.1647; abstract p.1643): TD +2.1% (ES 0.56), HID +27.2% (ES 1.79), HSR +23.5% (ES 1.67), SprD +40.2% (ES 1.61). Per minute: TD/min −1.1% (ES −0.27), HID/min +23.1%, HSR/min +19.8%, SprD/min +36.5% (Fig. 3).

**Match duration incl. added time, mean ± SD (Table 2, p.1649):** 96.3 ± 1.7 min (2015/16) … 98.4 ± 2.7 (2022/23), **101.3 ± 3.0 (2023/24)**, **99.5 ± 2.9 (2024/25)**. The 2023/24 jump follows IFAB's stricter added-time rule; 2024/25 fell after the rule was modified (p.1649). The authors link the TD changes to match length.

**TRACAB → Second Spectrum calibration (Fig. 1, p.1645; team totals, 2019/20 dual-tracked games, venue as random effect):**
- TD: 2S = 1,741 + 0.97 × TRACAB (r² 0.98, SEE 590 m)
- HSR: 2S = −587 + 0.99 × TRACAB (r² 0.94, SEE 197 m)
- SprD: 2S = −85 + 0.92 × TRACAB (r² 0.94, SEE 102 m)
So at typical team values 2S reads about 9–10% lower HSR and about 12% lower sprint distance than TRACAB (derived: HSR 6,000 → 5,353; SprD 2,000 → 1,755). Two optical 25 Hz systems, same stadiums, still differ by about 10% in high-speed zones.

**Fixture congestion (p.1649–1651, Figs 4–6):** congested minutes are 12–20% of all minutes per season for all players and 17–29% for top-six-club players. The 11 most-used players per team play about 70–73% of EPL minutes. The paper measures **exposure only**, not any effect on performance.

## Limitations
- Team totals only. No positions, no individuals, no within-match time course, no accelerations (optical tracking judged unreliable for them, p.1652).
- The provider switch coincides with the 2019/20–2020/21 jump in HID; the authors say it may account for part of the trend (p.1652).
- Team totals are sensitive to match length (2023/24) and to 11 v 11 (non-11 v 11 games removed).
- 2019/20–2020/21 include COVID seasons (empty stadiums, compressed calendar).
- Congestion results use capped minutes (96 / 128) and do not test injury or performance.
- Two authors are from an EPL club and one from the Premier League (competing interests, p.1653).

## Use in Running Battery
- Simulator: **the team-level target for a current EPL match.** Use 2024/25: team TD 113,891 ± 4,097 m, HID 9,611 ± 1,300 m, HSR 7,285 ± 931 m, SprD 2,326 ± 471 m, match length 99.5 ± 2.9 min. Our per-position table (synthesis §4.2) must add up to these. Per-minute values give a direct check of per player-slot output: 104 m/min, HID 8.8 m/min, sprint 2.1 m/min.
- Fatigue estimator: nothing direct. The ~10% HSR / ~12% sprint gap between two optical systems is a useful reminder that thresholds and filters matter more than they look.
- Agents / claims: supported: "EPL matches have become more intense: high-intensity distance per team is up about 27% and sprint distance about 40% since 2015/16 [Allen2025, Fig. 2]". Not supported from this paper: anything about individual positions, or that fixture congestion makes a player tired in a given match (exposure only). The "players pace themselves to save energy for high-speed efforts" idea (p.1650) is the authors' speculation, so label it as a hypothesis.
- Overlay / UI: "this match vs the league" gauge: put each team's live TD / HID / sprint (scaled by minutes played) against the 2024/25 mean ± SD. Fans get a percentile ("the most intense match this weekend") without any fatigue claim.

## Ideas or questions it raised
- Can we rebuild the missing position split from the team totals? Only with an assumed GK share and formation; see synthesis §4.2 for the consistency check.
- Online supplemental table 1 (season-to-season ES and CIs) is not in our PDF. It isn't needed, since the means and SDs are in Figs 2–3.
- Match length matters: a simulator that plays 90 or 96 minutes will undershoot team totals by 3–10%.
