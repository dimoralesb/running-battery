# Russell2011: Fatigue and soccer skills during a 90-min match simulation
Citation: Russell M, Benton D, Kingsley M (2011). The effects of fatigue on soccer skills performed during a soccer match simulation. Int J Sports Physiol Perform 6(2):221–233. https://doi.org/10.1123/ijspp.6.2.221
Type: lab study (field-based simulation on an indoor synthetic running track)
Context: Wales, UK. 15 youth (academy) players of an English Championship club (Swansea City acknowledged), 18.1 ± 0.9 y, 1.77 m, 71.1 kg, estimated VO2max 57.3 ± 0.7 mL/kg/min (mean ± SEM). Reliability sub-study: 10 players (16.9 ± 0.8 y), two 45-min trials. Soccer Match Simulation (SMS): 2 × 45 min + 15 min half-time, audio-paced. Skills filmed with 50 Hz video cameras; sprints by timing gates; HR (Polar S610); fingertip lactate. 20.3 °C, 65% humidity. No tracking system.
Evidence strength: low-medium. Well-controlled and repeatable, but n = 15 youth players, no opponents, ball delivered by a ramp, and no effect sizes reported. All values are mean ± SEM (not SD).

## Definitions and thresholds
- SMS block (p. 224): 4.5 min of 3 cycles of [3 × 20 m walk; alternating 15 m sprint or 20 m dribble; 4 s rest; 5 × 20 m jog at 40% VO2max; 1 × 20 m backward jog; 2 × 20 m strides at 85% VO2max], then a 1-min passing test and 1-min recovery. 7 blocks per half. Total 10.1 km, 56 passes, 16 shots, 21 dribbles.
- Shooting and passing (p. 224): ball released down a ramp at 2.3 m/s (8.3 km/h) to a 1.5 × 1.5 m action zone; random target lit 0.64 s before the ball arrives; no extra touch allowed. Pass targets 2.0 × 1.0 m at 4.2 m (short) and 7.9 m (long), each with a 0.50 × 0.25 m box. Shooting target: full goal 7.33 × 2.44 m, target lights 1.0 m inside each post and 0.5 m inside bar/ground (corners).
- Shooting tested 4 times (before and after each half, 4 shots each). Passing tested after every block (6 × 15-min bins reported). Dribble: 20 m slalom, cones 3 m apart.
- Precision = distance (cm) of the ball from the target centre (passes, shots) or from the cone (dribble). Success (%) = correct target/goal hit from inside the action zone; dribble success = cone negotiated without touching. Ball speed = average speed (m/s) from video (p. 225).

Rough Cohen's d (our calculation, SD = SEM × √15; NOT reported by authors): pass speed 0–15 vs 75–90 d ≈ 0.55; pass speed 1st vs 2nd half d ≈ 0.4; sprint speed d ≈ 0.8; shot precision pre vs post 2nd half d ≈ 0.6 (from figure values).

Timing: pass speed falls steadily from 30–45 min onward; shooting precision worsens within each half and recovers over half-time (pre-2nd-half value ≈ 90 cm is the best of the four) — a transient, within-half effect.

## Limitations
- No effect sizes, no CIs; results as mean ± SEM, which makes changes look tighter than SD would.
- Youth academy players (not senior professionals); n = 15; one session each.
- Closed skills: ball arrives from a ramp at a fixed 2.3 m/s, no opponent, no pressure, no choice of when to pass. Light cue adds a 0.64 s perceptual element only.
- Shot speed contradiction: the text says 2nd-half shots were slower (16.6 vs 17.3 m/s), but Fig. 4C (approx.) shows the highest value at post-2nd-half (≈17.5 m/s). We cannot resolve this from the paper.
- Shooting success did not fall; numerically it rose in the 2nd half (P = 0.072 between halves). "Less precise" here means larger distance from a corner target, not fewer shots on target.
- Pass speed loss could be a strategy (speed-accuracy trade-off, authors p. 230): players slowed passes and kept precision.

## Use in Second Wind
- Simulator: if any skill effect is simulated, the defensible one is a small drop in kick/pass ball speed late in the match (≈ −5 to −8% by 75–90 min) with NO drop in pass completion. Dribbling: no change. Sprint capacity: −5% over 15 m by the last 15 min (a lab value close to the hidden-truth top-speed drop we model).
- Fatigue estimator: supports top-speed decline (−5% in 15 m sprints) as a late-match fatigue marker; matches SilvaJR2018 (~3.5% slower sprint post-match).
- Agents / claims: allowed: "in a lab simulation, tired players struck the ball a bit slower and shot less precisely, but kept their pass success rate." Not allowed: "he missed the target because he was tired" or "fatigue cost him the pass" (success unchanged). Not allowed for dribbling at all.
- Overlay / UI: none.

## Ideas or questions it raised
- READING_LIST claim check: "Shooting precision and pass speed drop; dribbling doesn't" — correct. Missing nuance: pass precision and pass success did NOT drop; shooting success and (by ANOVA) shot speed did not change; no effect sizes are reported (we give rough d values above). "Effect sizes per skill" cannot be extracted from the paper as reported ES.
- Dambroz2022's text claims Russell found lower pass accuracy; that is wrong (Table 1 here).
- Shooting precision recovers over half-time: a nice example of transient vs cumulative fatigue. Our W′-balance-style hidden state also recovers at half-time; consistent.
- Ball speed is something our synthetic events could carry (pass speed), but the effect is small and not observable from player tracking alone.
