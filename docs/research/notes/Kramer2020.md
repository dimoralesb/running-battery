# Kramer2020: CS and D′ norms for athletic and non-athletic groups (running 3MT)
Citation: Kramer M, Thomas EJ, Pettitt RW (2020). Critical speed and finite distance capacity: norms for athletic and non-athletic groups. Eur J Appl Physiol 120(4):861–872. doi:10.1007/s00421-020-04325-5
Type: cross-sectional, normative (stanines), plus a "quasi meta-analysis" of published running 3MTs (Fig. 2).
Context: 43 athletic participants (16 soccer, 14 rugby, 5 hockey, 4 MMA, 4 track; 23 ± 4 y) and 25 non-athletic but active participants. Treadmill graded test at 1% grade with a verification bout (VO₂max, gas exchange threshold). Running 3MT on an outdoor track: wrist GPS (Polar V800) and a calibrated foot pod (Stryd) at 1 Hz; a bi-exponential curve fitted to speed; **D′ = area under the curve above CS** (not the 150 × (S150 − CS) shortcut).
Evidence strength: **medium.** Established journal; Crossref checked 2026-10-17 (the DOI ends in -5, not -3). The soccer players are not reported separately, the athletes' VO₂max is modest (50 ± 9 ml/kg/min), and the level of play is not stated.

## Key numbers (Table 1)
| | Athletic (n = 43) | Non-athletic (n = 25) |
|---|---|---|
| CS | **3.74 ± 0.50 m/s** (13.5 km/h) | 3.00 ± 0.56 |
| D′ | **208.5 ± 52.1 m** | 183.7 ± 63.5 |
| Speed at VO₂max (sVO₂max) | 4.48 ± 0.51 m/s | 3.76 ± 0.61 |
| VO₂max | 50.0 ± 8.6 ml/kg/min | 48.3 ± 7.6 |
| Smax in the 3MT | 8.49 ± 0.78 m/s | 7.13 ± 1.00 |
| Speed decay time constant τd | 35.2 ± 9.1 s | 38.0 ± 10.9 |

- CS / sVO₂max = 0.83 (athletic) and 0.80 (non-athletic) (derived, ratio of the means).
- D′ did **not** differ between athletes and non-athletes (difference −24.8 m, d = −0.43, p = 0.086) (Table 1).
- CS vs VO₂max: r = 0.70 in athletes, 0.62 in non-athletes; pooled r = 0.57 in the Results and 0.50 in the Discussion; slope 6.39 ml/kg/min per m/s (Results; Discussion; Fig. 4).
- Stanines for athletes (Table 3): CS "average" 3.59 m/s; D′ "average" 193 m, "very high" 266 m.
- The quasi meta-analysis (Fig. 2) gives grand means by group; the values are only in the figure. The "about 225 m for athletes" that Liu2021 cites is presumably from there; it is not in the text.

## Equations
Bi-exponential 3MT speed curve, S(t) = A_g(1 − e^(−t/τg)) … + A_d e^(−t/τd) + CS (Eq. 1, missing in the .md; see PDF). D′ = area above CS.

## Main message
The 3MT separates athletes from non-athletes mainly by CS and sprint speed, not by D′. Norms are given as stanines.

## Limitations and errors
- Soccer players pooled with rugby, hockey, MMA and track.
- 1 Hz wrist GPS and foot pod; bi-exponential fitting.
- Two different pooled CS–VO₂max correlations (0.57 and 0.50) and two D′ differences (−24.76 in Table 1, −24.27 in the text).
- Non-athletes and athletes had similar VO₂max but very different CS (Table 1; Discussion): VO₂max alone does not set CS.

## Use in Running Battery
- **CS prior (decision 13)**: an independent check of our "CS at 85% of the speed at VO₂max" assumption. Athletes run CS at about 83% of sVO₂max. With our professional derivation (VO₂max 57.6 ml/kg/min, 0.196 ml/kg/m [Modric2020]) the speed at VO₂max is 4.90 m/s and 83% of it is 4.07 m/s (derived), which still rounds to 4.1. No change.
- **CS spread**: VO₂max explains only 25–50% of CS between players (r 0.5–0.7). So a CS SD derived from the VO₂max spread alone (0.26 m/s from [Carpels2026]) is a floor; widening it to 0.3 was right, if anything still narrow (athletes of mixed sports: 0.50).
- **D′ prior (decision 1)**: field-sport athletes 208 ± 52 m, active non-athletes 184 ± 64 m. Our 180 ± 54 m sits half an SD below the athletes; no reason to raise it to ~225 (the figure-only grand mean).
- **Claims**: none.
