# DiSalvo2007: Performance characteristics by playing position (Spanish league + Champions League)
Citation: Di Salvo V, Baron R, Tschan H, Calderon Montero FJ, Bachl N, Pigozzi F. Performance characteristics according to playing position in elite soccer. Int J Sports Med. 2007;28(3):222-227. doi:10.1055/s-2006-924294
Type: match study
Context: **Spanish Primera División (20 matches) + UEFA Champions League (10 matches), not the Premier League.** Seasons 2002/03 and 2003/04, all at one stadium (Santiago Bernabéu, Madrid). 300 outfield players "profiled, including the home players, once only" (p.223). Positions: CD 63, ED (external/wide defenders) 60, CM 67, EM (external/wide midfielders) 58, F 52 (p.223). Optical: Amisco Pro v1.0.2, 8 fixed synchronised cameras, **25 Hz** ("25 measures a second") (p.223).
Evidence strength: medium for its time (300 players), but low for our use: old, non-EPL, a single stadium (one home team always included), and a different speed scheme.

## Limitations
- Not Premier League. One stadium (Bernabéu), so one home team is always in the sample. The phrase "including the home players, once only" suggests the home team's players were counted once, so most of the 300 are visiting players (unclear).
- 2002-2004 data, Amisco 1.0.2. Different thresholds from the EPL standard.
- **Internal inconsistency**: for CD, the speed bands in Table 2 add up to 10329 m, while Table 1 gives 10627 m (≈ 300 m difference). EM: 11874 vs 11990 m. Other positions match within ~30 m.
- Only halves; no 15-min or 5-min periods; no accelerations.
- Statistical reporting is loose (e.g. "p < 0.5" in the abstract and p.224; probably meant p < 0.05).

## Use in Running Battery
- Simulator: **sprint event structure**: ~17 sprints per match above 23 km/h, mean ~19 m per sprint (range ~10-33 m), by position (CD ~11, CM ~14, ED/EM/F ~20-22). This is the only source in this group with sprint counts by position. Distances with the ball are 1-2.5% of total: useful for the ball-carrier model. Treat these as old non-EPL numbers. Use them for structure and for ratios between positions, not as absolute targets.
- Fatigue estimator: halves show no drop at high speeds and a small drop at moderate speeds. This supports the view that whole-half comparisons are a weak fatigue signal; finer windows are needed.
- Agents / claims: do not cite as "Premier League". Correct label: "Spanish league and Champions League, 2002-04, Amisco".
- Overlay / UI: none.

## Ideas or questions it raised
- READING_LIST says "Old position profiles. Replaced by Morgans2025 / Allen2024". It should also say **Spanish La Liga + Champions League, not EPL**. Also, it is not truly replaced: neither Morgans2025 nor Allen2024 gives sprint counts or sprint length by position.
- Mean sprint length (19.3 m with a 23 km/h threshold) vs Bradley2009's derived ~7 m above 25.1 km/h: the effort length depends strongly on the threshold and on how the effort is defined (whole run vs only the part above the threshold). The simulator should model the whole run (acceleration phase + time above threshold) and compute counts with the target paper's definition.
- Do not confuse with Di Salvo et al. 2009 (Int J Sports Med 30:205-212, EPL high-intensity activity), which Kavanagh2024 cites as "Di Salvo [46]".
