# Mohr2003: Match performance and fatigue development in high-standard soccer (video time–motion)
Citation: Mohr M, Krustrup P, Bangsbo J (2003). Match performance of high-standard soccer players with special reference to development of fatigue. Journal of Sports Sciences 21(7):519–528. doi:10.1080/0264041031000071182
Type: match study
Context: 18 "top-class" players from one elite European team in the Italian league and Champions League (14 internationals) and 24 "moderate" professional players from the top Danish league; 2–7 competitive matches per player over two consecutive seasons, 129 match analyses; 13 top-class substitutes observed in up to 4 matches. Method: one VHS camera per player at the halfway line, ~15 m high, 30–40 m from the touchline; manual computerised coding by one experienced observer. Not a tracking system: no Hz; distances = time in category × assumed mean speed of the category. Positions: 11 central defenders, 9 full-backs, 13 midfielders, 9 attackers. (pp.519–521)
Evidence strength: medium. Classic and widely replicated concept paper, careful method for its time, but small sample, manual coding, category mean speeds instead of measured speed, era 1990s–2000s, statistics as mean ± SEM. Superseded for numbers by tracking studies (Leifsson2024).

| High-speed running | 18 | 5.0 | HIR |
| Sprinting | 30 | 8.33 | HIR (also reported alone as "sprinting") |
(p.520). These are **mean speeds of categories**, not thresholds. HIR here therefore starts around 15 km/h (4.2 m/s), much lower than the ≥ 5.0–5.5 m/s used by Schimpchen2020 and Leifsson2024.
- Peak 5-min HIR: the 5-min period with the most HIR, per player and match (fixed 5-min periods, p.520–521). "Next 5 min" compared with the mean of all other 5-min periods.
- Reliability: test–retest CV 1% TD; 2, 5, 3, 3% for walking, low-intensity, HIR, backward running (p.521).

**Recovery time constant**: not measurable here (fixed 5-min bins only). The authors cite unpublished data: sprint performance reduced after an intense first-half period but recovered by the end of the first half (p.526–527).

## Equations     (LaTeX, with variable definitions and units; mark "checked against PDF")
Distance per category (checked against PDF, p.520):
$$ d_c = t_c \cdot \bar{v}_c , \qquad TD = \sum_c d_c $$
t_c = total time in locomotor category c (s), \bar v_c = assumed mean speed of category c (m/s), d_c in m.
Coefficient of variation (checked against PDF, p.521):
$$ CV = \frac{SD(\Delta)}{\bar{x}} \times 100 $$
SD(Δ) = SD of the difference between repeated measurements, \bar x = mean (%).

## Limitations
- Manual video coding with assumed category speeds; sprint distance and HIR are estimates, not measured speed.
- Fixed 5-min and 15-min bins underestimate true peaks (rolling windows are higher; see Leifsson2024 and Schimpchen2020).
- Mean ± SEM, not SD (SEM is much smaller).
- Small, two-team design; "top-class" = one Italian club.
- No control for regression to the mean in the peak/next-5-min analysis; only 6 of 27 matches had a > 1 goal margin, used to argue against "match decided" effects (p.527).
- Inconsistent percentages between abstract and results for the last-15-min decline.

## Use in Running Battery
- Simulator: end-of-match decline target: HIR in the last 15 min ~25–30% below the first 15 min, sprint ~40% lower for top-class players; second half HIR ~10% below first half; temporary dip after the peak 5-min (−12%); dip at the start of the 2nd half (first 5 min ~20% lower than the first 5 min of the match). Substitutes should be fresher in the last 15 min (+25% HIR, +63% sprint). Positional base rates (table above) are dated; prefer modern tracking data if available.
- Fatigue estimator: supports three time scales: short post-peak, start of 2nd half, end of match. A single W′bal with one τ will not create the end-of-match decline; needs a slow component.
- Agents / claims: can cite as the origin of "temporary fatigue" and "end-of-match fatigue"; for numbers, use Leifsson2024 / Schimpchen2020. Substitute comparison is good evidence for agents ("fresh legs").
- Overlay / UI: none specific; maybe a "fresh sub" badge.

## Ideas or questions it raised
- READING_LIST check: description is fine ("origin of temporary and end-of-match fatigue; take numbers from Leifsson2024"). Note that "high-intensity running" here starts at ~15 km/h (4.2 m/s), so its numbers must not be mixed with ≥ 5.5 m/s HSR.
- Abstract "35–45% lower HIR in the last vs first 15 min" does not match the text/figure (~26–30% vs first 15 min; text range 14–45% is vs each of the first four periods for top-class). Cite the text numbers.
- "Peak 5-min then −12%" is a fixed-bin result; with rolling windows (Leifsson2024) the 5-min recovery after a 5-min HSR peak was −19%.
