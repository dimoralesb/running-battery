# SilvaH2025: Speed profile 10 s before and after each player's match peak speed (Brazilian Serie A, GNSS 10 Hz)
Citation: Silva H, Nakamura FY, Racil G, Gómez-Diaz A, Menezes P, Chamari K, Marcelino R (2025). Peak match sprinting speed during soccer matches: analysing the pre- and post-peak speed dynamics. Biology of Sport 42(1):193–200. doi:10.5114/biolsport.2024.136089 (published online 2024-08-08; PMC11694206)
Type: match study (short descriptive)
Context: one highly ranked Brazilian first-division team, 6 consecutive league matches, 2022 season; 20 elite male outfield players (27.8 ± 5.3 y): 4 CD, 3 FB, 5 CM, 5 WM, 3 FW; 90 match observations (one peak per player per match). GNSS WIMU Pro (RealTrack), **10 Hz**, FIFA-certified, same device per player (p.194).
Evidence strength: low–medium. Clean raw 10 Hz data and a simple design, but one team, 6 matches, small positional groups, no context, and it is a **kinematic description of the single top-speed effort**, not a fatigue or recovery study.

## Definitions and thresholds
- Match peak speed: player's highest speed in the match, accepted only if another effort within 1 km/h (0.28 m/s) of it occurred in the same match, and if > 25.2 km/h (7.0 m/s). Speeds > 44.45 km/h (12.35 m/s) removed as noise (p.194).
- Window: 10 s before to 10 s after the peak at 0.1 s steps; summarised per second (p.194).
- Statistic: paired mean differences between consecutive 1-s points (jamovi ESCI); Cohen's d: < 0.2 trivial, 0.2–0.6 small, 0.6–1.2 moderate, 1.2–2.0 large, 2.0–4.0 very large; CI crossing 0 = unclear (p.194–195).
- Sprint categories cited from Di Salvo: "explosive" vs "leading" sprints, HSR 19.8–25.2 km/h (5.5–7.0 m/s), sprint > 25.2 km/h (p.194).

- In m/s²: −4 s → −1 s mean gain ≈ +11.5 km/h in 3 s ≈ **+1.1 m/s²** average; +1 s → +3 s drop ≈ −12 km/h in 2 s ≈ **−1.7 m/s²** average (my conversion of Table 2; group means smooth individual peaks, so real individual values are higher).
- Starting speed of the peak effort ~10 km/h (2.8 m/s), "flying start"; ~5 s before and after the peak speeds are 5–10 km/h (p.195, Fig. 1). Players do not stop after the peak (+10 s: 6.6–7.5 km/h).
- Over −4 s to +3 s players gained ~10 km/h and lost ~16 km/h (p.197).
- Position differences in peak speed (Table 3, p.197): FB faster than CD by 2.50 km/h [1.12; 3.88] (d = 1.40 large); WM faster than CD by 1.55 [0.19; 2.93] (d = 0.69 moderate); FB faster than FW by 1.81 [0.25; 3.37] (d = 0.92 moderate); other pairs unclear.
- Match peak speeds are usually ~90% of test maximal speed (cited, p.197); elite players reached 92% of test peak in matches (cited, p.193).

## Limitations
- One team, 6 matches, one peak per player-match; FB n = 10 (3 players), FW n = 17 (3 players).
- Peak-aligned averaging produces a sharp symmetric-looking peak by construction; per-second means hide individual acceleration magnitude.
- GNSS 10 Hz speed at the peak may be smoothed/filtered differently by device; raw data with only a > 44.45 km/h filter.
- No context (score, phase, ball possession), no fatigue state, no comparison of early vs late peaks.
- The "range" values in the abstract (29.11–31.64 km/h etc.) are ranges of **position means**, not of individuals.

## Use in Second Wind
- Simulator: realistic shape of a maximal sprint: start from ~10 km/h (2.8 m/s), accelerate over ~4–5 s to peak, then decelerate faster (~2–3 s) to ~10–15 km/h, no full stop. Position peak speeds (8.1–8.8 m/s) are usable as priors for top speed in a Brazilian-league-like team (check against Premier League data).
- Fatigue estimator: tells us that top speed in matches is reached from flying starts and only for a moment; the estimator's "observed top speed" is a noisy, rare event and depends on space/context. It gives **no** information about how fatigue changes speed after the peak.
- Agents / claims: could support "players reach top speed from a jog, not from standing" and "they brake harder than they accelerate". Must not be used for "after his sprint he was tired".
- Overlay / UI: a 20-s sprint profile mini-chart for a highlighted run.

## Ideas or questions it raised
- READING_LIST check: listed as "Speed drop after a max sprint". This is misleading: the "drop" is the **deceleration within 3–5 s** of the peak (the end of the same run), not a fatigue-related drop in speed over the next minutes. The paper does not study fatigue or recovery at all. Also the author list misses the last author, Rui Marcelino.
- Useful for the simulator's sprint kinematics and for checking our acceleration–speed profile (Morin): accelerations from ~2.8 m/s.
- Recovery time constant: not applicable.

Conversion quality: good — Tables 1 and 2 converted correctly; Table 3 (pairwise position differences) garbled; figures not converted.
