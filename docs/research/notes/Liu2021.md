# Liu2021: Critical speed models from a shuttle 3-min all-out test in academy players
Citation: Liu KC, Sheard J, Frixou T, Milton P, Prato Luna E, Piatrikova E, Williams S, Parr J, Roe G, Kramer M (2021). Comparing critical speed modelling approaches and exploring relationships with match-play variables in elite male youth soccer players. South African Journal of Sports Medicine 33(1):1–6. doi:10.17159/2078-516X/2021/v33i1a9738 (PMC9924614)
Type: lab/field test study + small match study (cross-sectional)
Context: English Premier League academy, U16 (age 15.2 ± 0.2 yr, 171.3 ± 7.0 cm, 59.0 ± 7.8 kg); 13 recruited, 12 analysed (one excluded for pacing); mid-season; two 80-min matches (2 × 40 min, 15-min break); match variables only from the final 15 min of each half; data from 5 players who played both matches averaged (p. 2). GPS: STATSports Apex Pro, 18 Hz, for both test and matches (p. 2).
Evidence strength: low. n = 12 youth players, two matches, exploratory correlations, magnitude-based inference (a contested method), small regional journal.

## Definitions and thresholds
- Test: 30-m shuttle 3MT, 185 s all-out on grass, no time cues, after familiarisation trial 72 h earlier (p. 2).
- Three analysis methods (p. 1–2, Fig. 1): (1) Traditional: CS from the final 30 s; D′ from the first 150 s (power analogue printed as W′ = 150 s · (P150s − CP), p. 1); (2) Bi-ExpAverage: bi-exponential model on the average speed of each shuttle; (3) Bi-ExpMax-Speed: bi-exponential model on the peak speed of each shuttle (Kramer 2019, Excel sheet). The bi-exponential equation itself is NOT written in this paper.
- Extra bi-exponential parameters: Smax (max speed), tmax (time to Smax), τd (time constant of speed decline towards CS), Ad (amplitude of decline Smax − CS); fatigue index FI = speed reserve as % of Smax (p. 1–2).
- Match thresholds: HSR ≥ 5.5 m/s (19.8 km/h); sprint ≥ 7.0 m/s (25.2 km/h); accelerations ≥ 2.0 m/s²; individualised: HSR above own CS, sprint above own Smax; dwell 0.5 s (p. 2).

## Equations     (LaTeX, with variable definitions and units; mark "checked against PDF")
Checked against PDF text (p. 1). Only the traditional 3MT formula is given, in power form:
$$W' = 150\,\mathrm{s}\,(P_{150s} - CP)$$
$P_{150s}$ = mean power over the first 150 s (W); $CP$ = mean power over the final 30 s (W); $W'$ in J. Speed analogue (implied, p. 1): $D' = 150\,(\bar v_{0-150} - CS)$, with speeds in m/s and $D'$ in m.
The bi-exponential model equation is not given (refers to Kramer et al. 2019, Physiol Rep 7:e13993).

## Limitations
- n = 12 U16 players, two matches, last 15 min of each half only; no match context (score, tactics) (p. 5).
- Shuttle 3MT: 30-m shuttles cap speed (Smax 5.0–6.7 m/s, far below sprint top speed) and turns add cost; values depend on shuttle length (p. 5).
- The three methods are not interchangeable: CS differs by up to 0.78 m/s and D′ by up to 81 m depending on analysis (p. 4–5).
- Magnitude-based inference (MBI) used; many correlations, no multiple-testing correction.
- Text says differences between bi-exponential models were significant for all variables except tmax, but τd had p = 0.09 (Table 3).
- Youth D′ (59 m) much lower than adult field-sport norms (~225 m), possibly maturation (p. 5).

## Use in Second Wind
- Simulator: shows that even "measured" CS and D′ depend strongly on the test and analysis. Use as the lower end for young players only; for adult Premier League players prefer CS ~3.5–4.0 m/s and D′ ~150–250 m (Sutcliffe2025; Kramer 2020 via this paper). Make D′ vary widely between players.
- Fatigue estimator: whatever CS/D′ we estimate, uncertainty is at least ±0.2–0.8 m/s in CS depending on method — show this band.
- Agents / claims: may say "CS and D′ values change with the analysis method, so they are estimates". Must not use the correlations as evidence that D′ predicts late-match performance (n = 12).
- Overlay / UI: none.

## Ideas or questions it raised
- READING_LIST: "Small regional journal, youth players. Optional." Correct. Also note it is an EPL academy (club not named; one author is affiliated with Manchester United FC), 18 Hz STATSports.
- The bi-exponential τd (~34–38 s) describes speed decline during the 3MT, not D′ recovery. Do not confuse it with the D′bal τ.
