# Osgnach2010: Energy cost and metabolic power in elite soccer (equivalent-slope match analysis)
Citation: Osgnach C, Poser S, Bernardini R, Rinaldo R, di Prampero PE (2010). Energy cost and metabolic power in elite soccer: a new match analysis approach. Med Sci Sports Exerc 42(1):170–178. DOI: 10.1249/MSS.0b013e3181ae5cfd
Type: match study (method + descriptive match data)
Context: Italian Serie A (first division), elite, season 2007–2008; 56 matches; 399 players from 20 teams (age 27 ± 4 yr, mass 75.8 ± 5.0 kg, stature 1.80 ± 0.06 m). Only the "guest" teams playing at two host stadiums (Meazza, Milan; Franchi, Florence) were measured, so one player can appear up to 3 times (p. 172). Goalkeepers and substitutes "excluded" (p. 172), but Figs 6–8 still plot substitutes. Tracking: semi-automatic multiple-camera video system (SICS), 4 cameras at 25 Hz; typical error for total distance 1.0% (p. 172, citing Rampinini).
Evidence strength: medium. Large elite match sample, but the energy values are model estimates (no physiological criterion measure in this paper), the filter used is not reproducible, and the model was later shown to underestimate measured energy cost (Buchheit2015). No positional or per-half breakdown.

- "High power" threshold TP = 20 W/kg, stated to equal a VO2 of about 57 mL/kg/min above resting (p. 173). (Check: 20 W/kg × 60 s / 20.9 J/mL O2 ≈ 57 mL/kg/min, my calculation.)
- Equivalent distance (ED): distance the player would have run at a steady pace on grass with the same energy (Eq. 14, p. 173).
- Equivalent distance index (EDI) = ED / TD (Eq. 15).
- Anaerobic index (AI) = energy expended above TP / total energy (Eq. 16). Note: AI counts only the part of the energy above the 20 W/kg line, which is why AI (0.18) is much smaller than the share of energy spent while power was > 20 W/kg (~42%).
- All energy values are net (above resting) (p. 174 Table 3 header, p. 176).
- KT = 1.29: terrain factor, grass vs. compact treadmill terrain, from Pinnington & Dawson 2001 (p. 173).

Noise amplification (p. 172): with position noise SD σ at 25 Hz, speed noise SD = 25√2·σ ≈ 35σ and acceleration noise SD = (25√2)²σ = 1250σ (Eq. 9–10).

## Equations     (LaTeX, with variable definitions and units; mark "checked against PDF")
All checked against PDF (pp. 171–173, 175), and checked numerically against Table 2.

Equivalent slope and equivalent mass, as printed (Eq. 1–2, p. 171):
$$ES = \tan(90^\circ - \alpha), \qquad EM = \frac{g'}{g}$$
α = angle between the runner's body and the terrain (°); g = 9.81 m/s²; g' = vector sum of forward acceleration and g (m/s²). The paper does not print the explicit form in a_f; that is in diPrampero2005 (α = arctan(g/a_f), g' = sqrt(a_f² + g²)). Explicit form (follows from diPrampero2005 Eq. 4 and 8):
$$ES = \tan\!\left(90^\circ - \arctan\frac{g}{a_f}\right) = \frac{a_f}{g}, \qquad EM = \sqrt{\frac{a_f^2}{g^2} + 1} = \sqrt{ES^2+1}$$
a_f = forward acceleration (m/s², negative when decelerating). The simplification to a_f/g is exact (tan(90° − x) = 1/tan x) and matches the paper's own example: 5 m/s² ↔ ES ≈ +0.50 (p. 176).

Energy cost of uphill running at constant speed, Minetti 2002 (Eq. 3, p. 171):
$$EC(i) = 155.4\,i^5 - 30.4\,i^4 - 43.3\,i^3 + 46.3\,i^2 + 19.5\,i + 3.6$$
i = incline (tangent of slope, dimensionless); EC in J/kg/m; 3.6 J/kg/m = flat, compact terrain, constant speed. Valid range in Minetti's data: −0.45 to +0.45 (p. 176).

Energy cost of accelerated running (Eq. 4, p. 172), with terrain factor (p. 173, Table 2 note):
$$EC = \left(155.4\,ES^5 - 30.4\,ES^4 - 43.3\,ES^3 + 46.3\,ES^2 + 19.5\,ES + 3.6\right)\cdot EM \cdot KT, \qquad KT = 1.29$$
EC in J/kg/m. Eq. 4 as printed does not contain KT; KT is applied afterwards (p. 173: "values of EC obtained by equation 4 were multiplied by a constant (KT = 1.29)").

Metabolic power (Eq. 5, p. 172; Eq. 17, p. 175):
$$P = EC \cdot v \qquad [\mathrm{W/kg}]$$
v = running speed (m/s). Worked example in the paper: 3.6 × 4.44 × 1.29 ≈ 20 W/kg at 16 km/h constant speed (Eq. 17; the paper misprints the unit of v as m/s²).

Distance, speed and acceleration from positions at 25 Hz (Eq. 6–8, 11–13, p. 172):
$$\delta(n)=\sqrt{[x(n)-x(n-1)]^2+[y(n)-y(n-1)]^2},\quad d(n)=d(n-1)+\delta(n),\ d(0)=0$$
$$\hat d(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{186} h(k)\,d(n-k),\qquad \hat v(n)=\frac{\hat d(n)-\hat d(n-1)}{T},\qquad \hat a(n)=\frac{\hat v(n)-\hat v(n-1)}{T},\quad T=\tfrac{1}{25}\,\mathrm{s}$$
x, y = position (m); h(k) = 187 filter coefficients, chosen to remove "too fast" position changes (values not given). Note the filter is applied to cumulative distance d(n), not to x and y, so the "acceleration" is the rate of change of path speed (tangential), not of the velocity vector.

Noise (Eq. 9–10): σ_v = 25√2·σ ≈ 35σ; σ_a = (25√2)²·σ = 1250σ.

Summary parameters (Eq. 14–16, p. 173):
$$ED=\frac{W}{EC_C\cdot KT},\qquad EDI=\frac{ED}{TD},\qquad AI=\frac{\sum W_{TP}}{\sum W}$$
W = total net energy (J/kg); EC_C = 3.6 J/kg/m; W_TP = energy above the power threshold TP (J/kg); TD, ED in m.

## Limitations
- No criterion measure: energy values are model outputs only. Later validation (Buchheit2015) found the same model underestimates VO2-based energy cost by ~23–29% during efforts and ~85% during passive recovery.
- Model assumptions (p. 176): all mass at the centre of mass (limb motion ignored, so values are "minimal"); Minetti's polynomial is only measured up to ES = +0.45 and is extrapolated beyond (< 1% of match time); jumping, kicking, tackling, dribbling, air resistance and resting metabolism are ignored; KT = 1.29 is one value for all grass (muddy or snowy pitches should use a higher KT).
- Acceleration is the time derivative of filtered path speed. Changes of direction at constant speed add no cost in this model.
- Filter coefficients h(k) not published, so the exact numbers cannot be reproduced; results depend strongly on smoothing (noise × 1250 for acceleration at 25 Hz).
- Sample bias: only away teams at two stadiums; substitutes and goalkeepers said to be excluded but appear in Figs 6–8.
- No positional, per-half or per-15-min data, despite the brief asking for these. The paper itself says positional and fatigue analyses are future work (p. 177).
- Internal inconsistencies: the 20 W/kg constant-speed equivalent is given as 16 km/h (Eq. 17), "approximately 14 km/h" (p. 175 text); my calculation gives 4.31 m/s (15.5 km/h). Fig. 5 says 13 W/kg at 9 km/h constant speed; Eq. 4 × KT gives 11.6 W/kg.

## Use in Running Battery
- Simulator: can be the simulator's "true" energy bookkeeping only if we want the truth and estimator to share a model, which we do not (circularity, PLAN §3). Better: the simulator uses a richer physiology (W' / two-compartment) and only uses Table 1–3 as calibration targets for the movement it produces (time in speed bands, ~68% walking time, ~1.3% time > 6.1 m/s, ~1% time with |a| > 3 m/s², TD ≈ 10.9 km per 95 min, ~115 m/min).
- Fatigue estimator: main formula for "accumulated metabolic load". Inputs at 10 Hz: path speed v and tangential acceleration a from smoothed positions. Instantaneous P = EC(a/g)·EM·1.29·v, integrate to kJ/kg. Use the 20 W/kg threshold for "high-power distance" and the AI. Expected match total ≈ 61 ± 7 kJ/kg net; mean ≈ 10.7 W/kg (derived). At 10 Hz, noise grows 10√2·σ ≈ 14σ for speed and 200σ for acceleration (same reasoning as Eq. 9–10, my derivation), so smoothing is required before computing a.
- Agents / claims: allowed: "accelerations cost energy even at low speed; ~42% of energy is spent above 20 W/kg in Serie A 2007–08 (video, 25 Hz)". Not allowed: exact kJ values as a measurement, positional differences (not in this paper), or calling 20 W/kg "anaerobic" as fact (it is a fixed, non-individual threshold; paper says it should be individualised, p. 176).
- Overlay / UI: power bands 0–10 / 10–20 / 20–35 / 35–55 / > 55 W/kg as colour bands; "equivalent distance" (ED, EDI ≈ 1.13–1.33) is a fan-friendly number ("he ran 10.9 km, but it cost him as much as 13.2 km of steady running").

## Ideas or questions it raised
- READING_LIST check: "the equivalent-slope method and energy cost equation" is correct, and KT = 1.29 (grass) is confirmed. Imprecise points: (1) the paper writes ES = tan(90 − α) and EM = g'/g; the explicit a_f forms are in diPrampero2005, and ES simplifies to a_f/g; (2) the paper gives no positional breakdown and no mean metabolic power (≈ 10.7 W/kg is my derived number); (3) the tracking was SICS semi-automatic video, 4 cameras at 25 Hz, 56 matches, 399 players (guest teams only).
- Thresholds 35 and 55 W/kg cannot be reached at constant speed on grass: P = 4.644·v gives 35 W/kg at 7.5 m/s (27 km/h) and 55 W/kg at 11.8 m/s (impossible). So EP/MP bands are almost entirely acceleration events. Good fan story: "max power happens in the first steps of a sprint, not at top speed".
- My derived mean (≈ 10.7 W/kg net ≈ 31 mL O2/kg/min) implies ~55–60% VO2max for a 55–60 mL/kg/min player, lower than the ~70% VO2max from heart rate/temperature (Bangsbo2006). This agrees with Buchheit2015 (model misses recovery-phase cost). Worth an uncertainty note.
- Decelerations are treated as cheap (min EC ≈ 2.33 J/kg/m at ES ≈ −0.2 after × KT, my calc). The muscle-damage (eccentric) cost of braking is not metabolic and is missed; this matters for a fatigue estimator.
- Our 10 Hz synthetic data have known noise, so we can test how much the chosen smoothing changes P (sensitivity study for the uncertainty band).
