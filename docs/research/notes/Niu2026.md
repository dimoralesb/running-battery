# Niu2026: Technical performance in "extremely demanding" vs ordinary most demanding passages, by position (CSL)
Citation: Niu Z, Wang Z, Wang Y, Iván-Baragaño I (2026). Intra-positional technical performance under different physical demands in elite football: a comparison between most demanding passage and extremely demanding passage. Frontiers in Physiology 17:1900931. doi:10.3389/fphys.2026.1900931 (received 2026-06-05, accepted 2026-07-23, published 2026-08-06)
Type: match study (cross-sectional comparison of peak windows; plus a machine-learning classifier)
Context: Chinese Super League, professional, season 2021. 176 matches, 4,102 player observations from starting (non-substitute) outfield players: 3,664 MDP and 438 EDP observations. CD 975 (861 MDP / 114 EDP), FB 959 (882 / 77), CM 980 (868 / 112), WM 618 (565 / 53), FW 570 (488 / 82). Tracking: semi-automated multi-camera optical STATS Pro (8 cameras), 25 Hz (p. 3). Journal: **Frontiers** (use with care per our source rule).
Evidence strength: low-medium. Decent sample, but: Frontiers journal; independent t-tests / Mann–Whitney on repeated observations of the same players (no mixed model, despite a claim that repeated measures were "accounted for"); many tests without correction; mostly small ES; no context control; abstract misdescribes some variables; and it compares windows **during** peaks, not after them.

## Definitions and thresholds
- HIR: ≥ 19.8 km/h (≥ 5.5 m/s) (p. 3).
- MDP: per player per match, the **5-min rolling** window with the most HIR distance (p. 3).
- EDP: an MDP whose HIR distance exceeds the position mean + 1.65 SD of all MDPs for that position (claimed to be the "top 5%") (p. 3). The actual EDP share is 438/4,102 = 10.7%, not 5% (our calculation), so the MDP distribution is not normal or the threshold was applied differently. Numeric thresholds (m of HIR per 5 min by position) are not reported in this paper (they come from Niu et al. 2025).
- MDP group = the remaining (non-extreme) MDPs. So the comparison is **between different player-matches**: technical output inside an extreme peak window vs inside an ordinary peak window. **No post-peak window is analysed.**
- 28 technical variables (Table 1, p. 3–4). Prefix N. = number attempted; **prefix S. = number successful (a count, not a percentage)**. Short pass < 15 m, long pass > 25 m. Also ball touches, possessions, ball lost/won, clearances, tackles, forward pass length, running-with-ball duration and length, distributions, 50-50 challenges.
- Stats: K-S and Levene tests, then Student t or Mann–Whitney; Cohen's d only for significant variables; trivial < 0.2, small < 0.6, moderate < 1.2 (p. 4). Random Forest + SMOTE + SHAP on significant variables, 70/30 split, 5-fold CV (p. 4).
- From the cited earlier paper (Niu et al. 2025, quoted p. 2): EDPs have +18.6% total distance, +58.5% sprint distance, +61.4% sprint efforts vs MDPs.

## Equations     (LaTeX, with variable definitions and units; mark "checked against PDF")
EDP rule (p. 3, checked against PDF):
$$\text{EDP} \iff HIR_{MDP,i} > \mu_{pos} + 1.65\,\sigma_{pos}$$
$HIR_{MDP,i}$ = HIR distance (m, ≥ 5.5 m/s) in observation $i$'s 5-min MDP; $\mu_{pos}, \sigma_{pos}$ = mean and SD of MDP HIR distance for that position (m).

## Limitations
- **Not a post-peak study.** It compares technical counts inside extreme vs ordinary peak windows across different matches. Differences may reflect the type of match/phase that produces an extreme peak (e.g. chasing the game, open counter-attacking matches) rather than fatigue.
- No control of score, opponent, possession or ball-in-play (authors' limitation, p. 11).
- Repeated observations of the same players analysed with independent-sample tests; many comparisons, no multiplicity correction.
- **Abstract misdescribes variables**: it says CDs "increased short-pass accuracy (ES 0.62)", but the variable with d = 0.62 is N.ShortPass (number of short passes); S.ShortPass (d 0.66) is the number of successful short passes — a count. No accuracy % was tested. Same for "fewer successful long passes" (count). Abstract ES signs differ from tables (e.g. FB long passes "ES = 0.57" vs table −0.57).
- Table errors: CD N.Distrib EDP 3.7 < MDP 4.9 but t = +1.71; FB Shot MDP "0.2 ± 0.15" (likely 0.02); WM Shot t = −8.85 for 0 vs 0.13; "top 5%" vs actual 10.7%.
- Small EDP n for WM (53) and FB (77). Single season.
- Frontiers journal; 2 months from submission to publication.

## Use in Running Battery
- Simulator: weak evidence; at most a small, position-dependent shift in *choice* during extreme spells: fewer long passes (CD, FB, CM, WM), shorter forward passes (FB, CM), CM a few more ball losses (+0.3 per 5 min) and tackles, FW fewer duels/touches and shorter carries. Do not hard-code accuracy penalties from this paper.
- Fatigue estimator: none directly (no time course).
- Agents / claims: At most: "in his most extreme spells, a central midfielder tends to go long less often and loses the ball a bit more" — labelled as weak evidence (Frontiers, small effects, not controlled for context). Do NOT say "central defenders pass more accurately when exhausted": the data show more short passes, not a tested accuracy gain.
- Overlay / UI: none.

## Ideas or questions it raised
- READING_LIST.md check: "176 CSL matches" correct (2021 season, 4,102 observations). "Under extreme demand, central midfielders lose the ball more and complete fewer long passes" correct (counts, d 0.30 and 0.34). "Central defenders actually improve short-pass accuracy" is **wrong as stated**: CDs make MORE short passes (N.ShortPass d 0.62) and more successful ones as a count (d 0.66); accuracy was not tested (ratio of means 92% vs 83% is only our rough calculation). Also, READING_LIST and PLAN imply this is about after-peak decline; it is about **during** extreme vs ordinary peaks. Frontiers note correct.
- The EDP idea (a player-independent, position-level threshold for "extreme" spells) could be used by our estimator as a flag: "this spell was in the top ~10% for his position".
- The finding that technical changes are mainly *strategy* (shorter, safer passes) fits Fang2024: passing accuracy holds; choices and speed-dependent actions change.
