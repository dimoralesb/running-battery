# Fang2024: Physical and technical performance in and after the worst-case scenario (Chinese Super League)
Citation: Fang Z, Wang Z, Li X, Gómez MA, Liu H (2025). Physical and technical performance in and after the worst-case scenario in matches of the Chinese Super League of soccer. Biology of Sport 42(2):95–103. doi:10.5114/biolsport.2025.142642. Published online 2024-09-06 (hence "2024" in the file name); issue year 2025. Journal and DOI checked on PDF p. 95.
Type: match study
Context: Chinese Super League (CSL), professional, seasons 2019–2021. 576 matches, 13,298 observations from 970 players who started and finished the match (CD 214 players / 3,764 obs; FB 192 / 2,580; CM 242 / 3,414; WM 135 / 1,170; FW 187 / 2,370). Tracking: multi-camera optical system STATS Pro (Sport-Universal Process, Nice), 25 Hz, roof-level cameras (p. 96).
Evidence strength: high for the physical part (very large sample, mixed models with context covariates, effective-time data). Medium for the technical part (event counts per minute are small numbers in 5-min windows; several table errors; comparison is with the peak itself, not with normal play).

## Definitions and thresholds
- HIR: speed > 19.8 km/h = > 5.5 m/s (p. 96). Sprint: > 25.2 km/h = > 7.0 m/s.
- Worst-case scenario (WCS), Peak5: the **rolling** 5-min window with the most HIR distance for each player. Post5: the 5 min immediately after. If Peak5 ended < 5 min before half-time/full-time (incl. stoppage), the observation was dropped (p. 96).
- **All variables are computed in effective playing time (ball in play)**: count and distance variables were normalised per minute of effective playing time, e.g. TD (m/min) = distance (m) in effective time / effective time (s) × 60. Percentages and averages kept raw (p. 96). It is not fully clear whether the 5-min windows themselves are in clock time or effective time.
- Statistics: generalized mixed linear models (Poisson), fixed effects = match location, match result, team strength and opponent strength (difference of log end-of-season ranks); random effects = player and team (p. 96–97). Magnitude-based inference: ES = (mean Post5 − mean Peak5) / SD Peak5; < 0.2 trivial, 0.2–0.6 small, 0.6–1.2 moderate, 1.2–2.0 large, > 2.0 very large; 99% CI; "clear" if CI does not span both +0.2 and −0.2 (p. 97).
- Technical variables (Table 1, p. 97), 24 total: Pass, PassAcc, Short pass (< 15 m) and accuracy, Long pass and accuracy (Table 1 says long pass is "less than 25 m"; this must be a typo for "more than 25 m"), Forward pass (to a teammate in the opponent half) and accuracy, Running with ball (Rnwb, ≥ 3 touches), average speed and average length of running with ball, individual possessions, average speed when receiving the ball (Avspdrcvb), touches per possession, time per possession, Shot, Ball regains, Ball lost, Duel and Duel success %, Clearance and Clearance success %, Tackle made and Tackle success %.

## Equations     (LaTeX, with variable definitions and units; mark "checked against PDF")
Effect size (p. 97, checked against PDF):
$$ES = \frac{\bar{x}_{Post5} - \bar{x}_{Peak5}}{SD_{Peak5}}$$
$\bar{x}$ = model-estimated mean of a variable in each window (units of the variable); $SD_{Peak5}$ = SD in Peak5.

Per-minute normalisation (p. 96, checked against PDF):
$$TD\ (\mathrm{m\,min^{-1}}) = \frac{d_{eff}\ (\mathrm{m})}{t_{eff}\ (\mathrm{s})}\times 60$$
$d_{eff}$ = distance covered during effective (ball-in-play) time; $t_{eff}$ = effective playing time.

## Limitations
- Reference is the peak itself. Peak5 is by definition the player's most intense 5 min, so a large drop in Post5 is partly guaranteed by selection (regression to the mean). The paper gives no match-average HIR, so we cannot tell whether Post5 is below the player's normal level or just back to normal.
- Event variables are rare in a 5-min window (e.g. 0.03 tackles/min, 0.01 shots/min); most per-window values are 0 or 1, so accuracy % in a 5-min window is very noisy (SDs ±30–60 points).
- Table errors: Table 3 CD forward-pass row (8.8 ± 9.7 → 0.82 ± 8.3) is a copy of the CD sprint values from Table 2; CD running-with-ball speed (10.3) and length (4.3) look swapped relative to other positions; long pass defined as "< 25 m" (typo). Units of running-with-ball speed and receiving speed not stated.
- Effective-time normalisation removes stoppage effects on per-minute rates, which is a strength; but tactical state (score at that moment, possession) is only controlled at match level (final result, team/opponent strength), not minute by minute.
- No accelerations/decelerations; only 5-min window; formations pooled (p. 101–102).
- CSL, 2019–2021 (COVID-affected 2020 season); generalisability to Premier League unknown.

## Use in Second Wind
- Simulator: after a 5-min peak, (a) scale HIR down sharply for the next 5 min (Post5 HIRD ~7.6 m/min vs 34 in peak, efforts 0.54 vs 1.9 per min); (b) make each remaining HIR effort shorter (−23% length, −32% duration) and a bit slower (−3% mean speed, i.e. ~6.3 → 6.1 m/s); (c) carries: −26% count, −18% length, −21% speed; receiving speed −20%; (d) leave pass/duel/tackle success unchanged after a peak (trivial changes).
- Fatigue estimator: per-effort features (mean HIR speed, length, duration) drop after peaks, not just volume. Useful second signal: mean speed of HIR efforts (−3%) and receiving speed.
- Agents / claims: Supported: "after his most intense spell he carried the ball shorter and slower, and received at lower speed." Not supported: "he misplaced passes / lost duels because he was tired" (success rates trivially changed). Must not present −77% HIR as "77% below normal"; it is vs his own peak.
- Overlay / UI: show post-peak drop relative to the peak (Fang) and relative to match average (Ju2022) as separate lines if shown at all.
