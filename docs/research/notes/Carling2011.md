# Carling2011: Physical decline vs skill-related performance in professional matches
Citation: Carling C, Dupont G (2011). Are declines in physical performance associated with a reduction in skill-related performance during professional soccer match-play? J Sports Sci 29(1):63–71. https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2010.521945
Type: match study
Context: France, LOSC Lille (French Ligue 1, top division). 11 midfielders (central and wide) who played full matches; 35 Ligue 1 + 2 UEFA Europa League games over three seasons (2007–08: 16; 2008–09: 15; 2009–10: 6); 25 home / 12 away; 60 player-match observations, median 9 games per player (range 2–24). Successive-games sub-study: 7 midfielders, 3 games in ≤7 days (December 2009–10). Tracking: AMISCO Pro (semi-automatic multi-camera optical), 10 Hz; ball actions coded by AMISCO analysts. Injury/added time excluded.
Evidence strength: medium. Real elite matches with optical tracking (better than lab), but one team, one position, 11 players, coarse event counts, and many comparisons with low power.

## Definitions and thresholds
- High-speed running (HSR): > 14.4 km/h = 4.0 m/s (abstract; p. 65).
- Peak 5-min period: the 5-min block with the most HSR for each player, using fixed blocks (0–5, 5–10 min…) (p. 65; authors note fixed blocks may under- or over-estimate peaks, p. 70).
- Skill-related measures (AMISCO definitions): total passes, % completed passes, ball possessions, possessions gained, possessions lost, touches per possession, duels, % duels won; also shots and % shots on target for the successive-games analysis (p. 65).
- Ball-in-play time per period was also measured (p. 65).
- Stats: Wilcoxon (halves), Friedman + Tukey (intervals), Cohen ES: 0.2 small, 0.5 medium, >0.8 large (p. 65).

Their answer (pp. 68, 70): physical decline across halves and 15-min intervals came WITHOUT a drop in skill frequency or proficiency. Only the final 5 min showed fewer passes, possessions and duels (volume), with pass completion unchanged (quality). No skill change after the peak 5-min period. No change across 3 games in a week.

## Limitations
- One team, midfielders only, 11 players, 60 observations (players repeated; not clear if handled as repeated measures).
- Coarse event counts (2–3 passes per 5 min) and very large SDs (pass success ±30%) give low power; "no difference" is not "no effect".
- Final 5 min: ball in play fell from 198.8 to 175.3 s (−12%), so fewer actions partly reflects less live play, not only fatigue. Per minute of ball in play, passes are still lower (2.8 / 3.31 min ≈ 0.85/min vs 2.1 / 2.92 min ≈ 0.72/min; our calculation).
- Peak periods use fixed 5-min blocks (no rolling window), and skills were counted over 5 min, while lab data suggest fatigue effects on skill may fade within ~2 min (authors, p. 69).
- No context: score line, tactics, opponent quality, pressure on the ball carrier (authors, p. 70).
- AMISCO data from 2007–2010; HSR threshold 14.4 km/h (lower than the 19.8 km/h often used today); not comparable to SkillCorner broadcast tracking or modern optical values without adjustment.

## Use in Running Battery
- Simulator: targets for a Ligue 1 midfielder: ~126 → 121 m/min total distance 1st vs 2nd half; HSR (>4.0 m/s) ~31 → 27 m/min from first to last 15 min; peak 5-min HSR ~48 m/min followed by ~29 m/min. Keep pass completion flat across periods (≈66–71% in every 15-min bin). Allow ~20–25% fewer passes/possessions in the last 5 min, mostly via less ball-in-play time.
- Fatigue estimator: confirms that the physical signal (HSR per period, post-peak drop) is visible in 10 Hz tracking even when event-level skill is not. The estimator should rely on running, not on events.
- Agents / claims: allowed: "his high-speed running fell after that intense spell" and "players are on the ball less in the closing minutes". Not allowed: "his passing accuracy dropped because he was tired" (no change in completion at any time scale). Not allowed: "the busy week made him worse" (no change across 3 games in 7 days).
- Overlay / UI: a period chart with HSR bars and a flat pass-completion line would visually make the point "the legs drop, the passing doesn't".

## Ideas or questions it raised
- READING_LIST claim check: "Asks our exact question in professional matches. If the link is weak, we say so." Correct and the answer is: weak/no link for quality; small link for volume in the last 5 minutes only, confounded by ball-in-play time. League confirmed as French Ligue 1 (LOSC Lille), system AMISCO Pro 10 Hz, n = 11 midfielders, 37 games.
- Authors suggest looking at skills in the first 2 min after a peak. Our synthetic data could do exactly this with a rolling window (but our synthetic truth would only reflect what we put in).
- Pacing: midfielders may hold back to keep skill output (authors, p. 68). Consistent with SilvaJR2018 (smaller test losses after real matches than after simulations).
