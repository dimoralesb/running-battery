# Mohr2005: Fatigue in soccer – a brief review (when it happens and why)
Citation: Mohr M, Krustrup P, Bangsbo J (2005). Fatigue in soccer: a brief review. Journal of Sports Sciences 23(6):593–599. doi:10.1080/02640410400021286
Type: review (narrative, mostly the authors' own studies)
Context: summarises Mohr2003 ("Mohr et al. 2003a": top-class Italian-league team, video time–motion, n = 18; 15-min distribution n = 93 observations), Krustrup et al. "2003a" (= the study later published as Krustrup2006: Danish 4th-division friendlies, muscle biopsies and sprint tests, n = 16–20), Mohr et al. 2004a (muscle temperature in a friendly game, n = 8–16), Krustrup et al. 2003b (Yo-Yo IR1 lab test). No new data.
Evidence strength: low–medium as a source of numbers (secondary, small studies, partly "unpublished observations"); useful and authoritative as a source of **mechanism wording**. Mechanisms for temporary fatigue are explicitly hypotheses.

## Definitions and thresholds
- Fatigue: "failure to maintain the required or expected power output" (Edwards 1983; p.593).
- Three fatigue phases (abstract, p.593; summary p.598): (1) temporarily after short intense periods in both halves; (2) at the start of the second half; (3) towards the end of the game.
- HIR thresholds as in Mohr2003 (video categories, ~15 km/h and up).
- Sprint test in Fig. 2: three 30-m sprints with 25 s active recovery (the original Krustrup study used five; see Krustrup2006).

## Mechanism summary (for agent wording)
| Fatigue type | When | Likely mechanism (as stated) | Ruled out / weak |
|---|---|---|---|
| Temporary | minutes after short intense spells, both halves | disturbed muscle ion homeostasis: K⁺ accumulation in the muscle interstitium, impaired excitation of the sarcolemma (hypothesis; "little is known about potassium turnover during a soccer game") | not directly linked to muscle glycogen, lactate, acidity (pH) or creatine phosphate breakdown |
| Start of 2nd half | first ~5 min after half-time | drop in muscle temperature (~2 °C) during a passive half-time; possibly slower O₂-uptake kinetics | — |
| End of match | last ~15 min | glycogen depletion in individual fibres (about half of type I and IIa fibres empty); in heat: dehydration and hyperthermia, reduced cerebral function | hypoglycaemia (blood glucose stays normal); core temperature not different between halves in normal climate |

## Limitations
- Narrative review by the same group; mostly self-citation; several "unpublished observations".
- Mechanisms for temporary fatigue are speculative (K⁺ hypothesis).
- r = 0.41 for muscle lactate vs sprint decline (cited from the 2003 abstract) conflicts with the final paper Krustrup2006 (no significant correlation).
- Figures are the only source of many values (approximate).
- Old (2005); newer reviews may update mechanisms.

## Use in Running Battery
- Simulator: model three separate effects: fast temporary fatigue after intense spells (recovers within minutes), a short "cold restart" dip in the first ~5 min of the 2nd half, and a slow end-of-match decline (glycogen), stronger in heat. Substitutes enter fresh.
- Fatigue estimator: the start-of-2nd-half dip is not caused by energy depletion, so the estimator should not read it as low battery; consider a separate "half-time cool-down" flag.
- Agents / claims: safe wording: "after an intense spell, the muscles need a few minutes to recover; this is probably linked to ion balance in the muscle (potassium), not lactic acid"; "late in the game many muscle fibres run low on glycogen"; "players often start the second half slower because their muscles cooled down at half-time." Avoid: "lactic acid build-up made him slow" (contradicted, p.594).
- Overlay / UI: half-time "muscle cooled" note in the first 5 min of the 2nd half; end-of-match "fuel" note.

## Ideas or questions it raised
- READING_LIST check: "When and why fatigue happens: after intense spells, start of 2nd half, end of match" – correct. Add that the temporary-fatigue mechanism is a hypothesis, and that lactate/pH/PCr/glycogen are explicitly not the cause.
- Could add a heat factor (match temperature) to the simulator as a scenario; evidence here is only qualitative.
- The start-of-2nd-half dip gives a test for our estimator: a pure energy-balance model would predict players are **fresher** after half-time; data say output is **lower** for ~5 min. Agents should explain it correctly.
