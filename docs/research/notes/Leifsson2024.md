# Leifsson2024: Peak intensity periods, temporary fatigue and minute-by-minute recovery (Danish Superliga)
Citation: Leifsson EN, Krustrup P, Mohr M, Randers MB (2024). Effect of peak intensity periods on temporary fatigue and recovery kinetics in professional male football. Journal of Sports Sciences 42(9):769–775. doi:10.1080/02640414.2024.2364135 (open access, CC BY-NC-ND)
Type: match study
Context: Danish Superliga (top tier, Denmark), professional men, 3 consecutive seasons 2015/16, 2016/17, 2017/18; all matches. 479 players (range 478–480); 9585–9671 match observations for high-speed running (HSRD), 9054–9249 for sprint distance (SpD), 6042–6111 for acceleration distance (AccD; fewer because of a data error). Only players who completed the full match. Tracking: semi-automatic multi-camera optical system (Prozone, Stats LLC). **The sampling rate (Hz) is not stated in the paper.** (p.770, PDF p.3)
Evidence strength: high for the descriptive minute-by-minute pattern (very large, multi-team, multi-season, rolling windows, per-minute resolution). Medium for the causal claim "fatigue": no physiological measures, no control for regression to the mean (selecting the maximum window guarantees lower values afterwards), no position or context breakdown, effect sizes trivial to small. Data owned by the league, not shareable.

## Definitions and thresholds
| Item | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| HSRD | distance at speed > 5.5 m/s (19.8 km/h) (abstract writes ≥) | p.769 abstract; p.770 Methods |
| SpD | distance at speed > 7.0 m/s (25.2 km/h) | p.769–770 |
| AccD | **distance** covered while accelerating > 3 m/s² (not a count of accelerations; decelerations not analysed) | p.770 |
| Peak periods P1, P2, P5 | most intense 1-, 2-, 5-min **rolling** window, found separately for each variable (an HSRD peak and an AccD peak need not coincide) | p.770 |
| Recovery periods R1..R5 | the 5 consecutive 1-min bins right after the peak window (method of Fransson et al. 2017) | p.770 |
| Peak timing rule | the peak must end no later than min 40 (1st half) or min 85 (2nd half), so the 5-min recovery stays inside the half | p.770 |
| "Mean match" per minute | match total **minus** peak and recovery periods, divided by 84, 83 or 80 min (for P1, P2, P5). So the reference excludes the peak itself. | p.770 |
| Stats | mean ± SD; normality by Kolmogorov–Smirnov; paired t-test / one-way ANOVA, or sign test for non-normal data; ES r = Z/√N (0.1 small, 0.3 medium, 0.5 large; Fritz 2012) | p.770 |

- Between-player range of peak rates: HSRD 5.9–142.4 (P1), 2.9–89.7 (P2), 1.2–51.6 (P5) m/min; SpD 8.4–139.5, 4.7–89.8, 1.9–44.5 m/min (p.772).
- Longer peaks give slower recovery (p.770): HSRD in R1₁, R2₁, R3₁ (after P1) was 29%, 32%, 11% higher than in R1₅, R2₅, R3₅ (after P5) (ES 0.07, 0.05, 0.04); HSRD in R1₂..R5₂ (after P2) was 20, 25, 16, 6, 1% higher than after P5. HSRD in R2₁ was 6% higher than in R2₂ (ES 0.06).
- Peak period share of playing time ~1, ~2, ~6% (p.771).
- Superliga P1/P5 HSRD (62/110 m) is similar to EPL, Championship and LaLiga values and higher than Norwegian and Spanish Segunda B (p.771, cited studies).

**Recovery time constant (my own derivation, not in the paper).** Fitting a single exponential D(k) = D₁·e^{−(k−1)/τ} to the abstract's deficits (k = recovery minute):
| Series | Fit points | τ (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| HSRD after P1 | 29% → 6% (R1→R2), then ~2% plateau | ≈ 0.6 min (≈ 40 s) |
| HSRD after P2 | 35% → 11% (R1→R2), then ~0–3% | ≈ 0.9 min (≈ 50 s) |
| HSRD after P5 | 45, 29, 13, 8, 4% (log-linear over R1→R5) | ≈ 1.6–1.7 min (≈ 100 s) |
| SpD after P5 | 39% → 12% (R1→R5) | ≈ 3.4 min (≈ 200 s), not recovered at R5 |
These are behavioural (output) recovery constants in 1-min bins, not physiological capacity constants.

## Equations     (LaTeX, with variable definitions and units; mark "checked against PDF")
Effect size (checked against PDF, p.770):
$$ r = \frac{Z}{\sqrt{N}} $$
Z = standardised test statistic (dimensionless), N = number of observations. 0.1 small, 0.3 medium, 0.5 large.

Mean match per-minute reference (checked against PDF, p.770):
$$ \bar{x}_{\text{match}} = \frac{X_{90} - X_{\text{peak}} - X_{\text{rec}}}{90 - L - 5} $$
X₉₀ = match total distance in the category (m), X_peak, X_rec = distance in the peak window and the 5-min recovery (m), L = peak length (1, 2 or 5 min). Denominator 84, 83, 80 min.

## Limitations
- No physiological measures; authors say they cannot rule out pacing or tactical changes (p.774).
- Regression to the mean / selection bias: choosing the single highest window per match makes the next minutes lower by construction. Not discussed or controlled. Part of the "fatigue" curve is statistical.
- Very skewed data (medians all 0 in recovery minutes, ~65% zeros); means and SDs (SD > mean) describe a zero-inflated variable.
- Trivial-to-small ES; huge sample makes 0–3% differences "significant".
- One peak per player-match per variable; no positions, halves, score or opponent breakdown.
- AccD counts only acceleration distance > 3 m/s²; decelerations not analysed. AccD data had an error in some matches (fewer observations).
- Tracking Hz not reported; Prozone semi-automatic, 2015–18; not directly comparable with GPS (Schimpchen2020) or modern optical data.
- Peaks restricted to end ≤ min 40/85, so late-half peaks (possibly the most fatiguing) are excluded.

## Use in Running Battery
- Simulator: use the Table 1 / abstract curves as calibration targets for post-peak high-speed output: after a 5-min peak, HSR (> 5.5 m/s) at −45, −29, −13, −8, −4% of the player's normal per-minute rate in minutes 1–5; after 1–2-min peaks, about −30% in minute 1 and back to normal by minute 2–3. Sprint (> 7 m/s) recovers more slowly after long peaks (still −12% at min 5). Acceleration distance (> 3 m/s²) must **not** be suppressed after peaks (it stays ~1.6–1.9× match average) – accelerations cluster in busy phases. Remember the target already contains selection artefact, so calibrate with the simulator's own fatigue switched off first (see Ideas).
- Fatigue estimator: rolling 1-, 2-, 5-min HSR and sprint windows vs the player's own match peak; flag "post-peak" for ~2 min after a 1–2-min peak and ~5 min after a 5-min peak. Do not use acceleration counts as a fatigue signal in the minutes after a peak. A W′-balance τ chosen to reproduce these curves would be ~1–2 min for HSR and ~3–4 min for sprint after long peaks (behavioural fit, see table).
- Agents / claims: supported: "in the minute after his most intense spell, a typical player covers ~30–45% less high-speed distance than usual; after short spells it is back to normal within ~2 min, after a 5-min spell it takes ~5 min; sprint distance stays lower longer." Not supported: "he can't accelerate after the spell" (AccD was higher), or any claim about individual players from this average data.
- Overlay / UI: a post-peak "recovery bar" filling over ~2 min (short spell) or ~5 min (5-min spell). Show it as typical-player behaviour, with wide spread (SD > mean).

## Ideas or questions it raised
- READING_LIST check: "479 players, 3 Danish Superliga seasons, rolling averages" – correct. "HSR drops by up to 45% in the next minute and recovers over ~5 min" – correct for 5-min peaks only; after 1- and 2-min peaks HSR is back within ~2–3 min. Sprint after 5-min peaks is **not** recovered at 5 min (−12%). "Accelerations go up after the peak" – imprecise: AccD in the recovery minutes is higher than the **whole-match average** (+23% to +94%), but this is after an **AccD peak**, not after a high-speed-running peak, and the authors read it as accelerations clustering in the same phases, not as an increase caused by the peak. AccD in R1 is still the lowest of the 5 recovery minutes. Thresholds (≥5.5 m/s, ≥7 m/s, ≥3 m/s²) are correct; AccD is distance, not number of accelerations.
- Regression to the mean: run Leifsson's exact pipeline on synthetic matches with fatigue turned OFF to measure how big the "post-peak drop" is from selection alone. The true fatigue effect is the observed curve minus this baseline. This would be a strong, honest validation slide.
- The authors link the fast recovery to PCr resynthesis and muscle ion (Na⁺/K⁺) handling – speculation only (p.772).
- Behavioural τ (~1–2 min for HSR) is much shorter than typical W′bal recovery τ from cycling (Skiba2012; check that note). Our simulator may need a fast component (temporary fatigue) plus a slow component (end-of-match), i.e. the two-compartment design in PLAN.md.
