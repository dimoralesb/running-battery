# Rampinini2008: Match-related fatigue and short-passing ability in young players
Citation: Rampinini E, Impellizzeri FM, Castagna C, Azzalin A, Ferrari Bravo D, Wisløff U (2008). Effect of match-related fatigue on short-passing ability in young soccer players. Med Sci Sports Exerc 40(5):934–942. https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0b013e3181666eb8
Type: lab study / field experiment (quasi-experimental control-period design; passing test done beside the pitch during two friendly matches, plus a 5-min running simulation)
Context: Italy (Mapei Sport Research Center). 16 junior players from a professional club (17.6 ± 0.5 y, 174 ± 7 cm, 68 ± 6 kg). Two unofficial matches, same two teams and referee; 8 players (4 per team) tested per match. LSPT data at half-time and full-time for 15 players; within-half data for 11. Grass pitch; LSPT on artificial surface next to it; 12–16 °C, 35–40% humidity. No tracking: HR every 5 s (Polar), blood lactate, RPE (CR10), and video notational counts (Sony digital 8 camera). Radar (Stalker ATS) only during the 5-min simulation.
Evidence strength: medium-low. Small sample (11–16 juniors), friendly matches, LSPT is a closed drill. Strong within-study effect on accuracy, but match technical counts show no significant change.

## Definitions and thresholds
- LSPT (first version, p. 935): 16 passes in clockwise order against a wooden target (30 × 250 cm) with coloured zones, as fast and accurately as possible. Penalties: +5 s missing target or wrong target; +3 s hitting the target but not a coloured area; +2 s yellow area; +1 s blue area; +2 s passing from outside the area; +2 s ball touching a cone; +1 s per second over 43 s. Bonus −1 s for hitting the 10-cm purple strip.
- Scores: LSPT time (execution, s), LSPT penalties (s), LSPT total performance (time + penalties − bonus, s). Higher = worse.
- Test times in matches: 40, 42, 44, 46 min (end of first half) and 85, 87, 89, 91 min (end of second half); subset also at 22–26 and 67–71 min (p. 935). Players left the pitch to do the test, one per team at a time.
- High-intensity simulation (HIS, p. 936): 10 × 40 m shuttles (20 m + 180° turn), five at 16 km/h = 4.44 m/s and five at 19 km/h = 5.28 m/s, 25 s recovery alternating 0, 5 km/h (1.39 m/s) and 7 km/h (1.94 m/s). Work:rest ≈ 1:3. Based on the most demanding match phase in Withers et al. and the 5-min period of Mohr et al.
- Match technical scores (video, p. 937): involvements with the ball; short passes (<40 m); successful short passes; long passes (>40 m); successful long passes.
- Effect size: η² (0.01 small, 0.06 medium, >0.15 large) (p. 937).

## Limitations
- Small sample of juniors (11–16) from one club; two friendly matches.
- The LSPT is done off the pitch with no opponents. Players left the match to do the test (duration of the absence not reported), which may itself change fatigue and focus.
- No measure of physical output in the matches (no tracking, no sprints), so the "relation to physical decline" is only via HR, lactate and RPE.
- Accuracy (penalties) is where almost all the loss is: penalties +40–60% but LSPT time only +4–5% (authors, p. 940).
- Match notational counts (video, subjective coding) show no significant drop and stable success rate; authors say these counts are crude (p. 940).
- η² values from a repeated-measures ANOVA are not Cohen's d. Our d values are rough.

## Use in Running Battery
- Simulator: if we add any lab-based pass-accuracy penalty, treat Rampinini as an UPPER BOUND for junior players in a closed drill: error-related penalties rise ~40% by half-time and ~60% by full-time; speed of play ~4–5% slower. Because the in-match success rate did not drop (75% vs 79%), keep the in-game pass completion penalty very small (a few percentage points at most) and let fitness (our hidden aerobic capacity) reduce it.
- Fatigue estimator: nothing direct. Shows HR mean falls 174 → 165 bpm and lactate 5 → 3 mM in the 2nd half, consistent with lower 2nd-half work.
- Agents / claims: allowed: "in passing tests, junior players made clearly more passing errors after a match half than when fresh" (lab). Allowed: "fitter players lost less passing accuracy after the same hard running". Not allowed: "he misplaced the pass because he was tired" — the same study found no drop in match pass success.
- Overlay / UI: none.

## Ideas or questions it raised
- READING_LIST claim check: "Passing quality drops with fatigue" is true for the LSPT drill (penalties +43% / +62%) but NOT for in-match passing in the same players (short-pass success 12/16 vs 11/14, η² 0.01). Pass volume dropped slightly (n.s.). The "size of the drop" is test-specific, not a match pass-accuracy penalty.
- The 5-min HIS gives a clean transient-fatigue dose: 273 m above 4.17 m/s in 5 min (≈ 55 m/min high-speed) → +20% penalties. This could calibrate a "post-peak" transient skill penalty in the simulator, with the strong caveat of lab drill.
- Fitness moderates the decline (r ≈ −0.6). Our hidden profile (CS, D′) could scale any skill penalty.
