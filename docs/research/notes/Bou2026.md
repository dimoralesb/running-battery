# Bou2026: A-S profiles from broadcast video as fatigue indicators (preprint)
Citation: Bou X, Correger N, Cloots A, Gavage C, Giancola S, Schwartz C, Delvaux F, Cloots R, Van Droogenbroeck M, Cioppa A (2026). Towards athlete fatigue assessment from association football videos. arXiv:2604.05636v1 [cs.CV], 7 Apr 2026. PREPRINT (not peer reviewed).
Type: preprint (computer-vision method + feasibility study)
Context: SoccerNet Game State Reconstruction (GSR) test set: 49 video clips of 30 s, 773 athletes in ground truth; 746 predicted athletes, 578 matched to ground truth (p. 4). Plus one full 45-min broadcast segment from the Swiss Super League (no ground truth) (p. 4, p. 7–8). Tracking: monocular broadcast video → Broadcast2Pitch GSR method → pitch coordinates, 25 fps (p. 4–5). Ground truth = annotated positions in SoccerNet-GSR (not GPS).
Evidence strength: low. Preprint; fatigue claim rests on ONE player in ONE 45-min segment without ground truth; "ground truth" speed is itself derived from annotated video positions.

## Definitions and thresholds
Processing pipeline (Sec. 3, p. 3–4):
- Gap filling: linear interpolation for gaps ≤ 3 frames (0.12 s at 25 fps); longer gaps left missing.
- Position smoothing: Kalman filter (constant velocity; chosen) or Savitzky–Golay (window 9, order 2).
- Speed and acceleration by central differences over a neighbourhood of ±l_n frames (Eqs. 4–6). Chosen l_n = 20 (window reported as 1.64 s). Larger l_n avoided to keep local dynamics.
- Speeds > 15 m/s removed. Acceleration then smoothed with a central moving average over 20 values (following Pons et al. 2021).
- A-S profile exactly as Morin2021: speeds from 3 m/s to the athlete's max, 0.2 m/s bins, 2 highest accelerations per bin, linear fit, remove outliers outside 95% CI, refit (p. 4).
- Fatigue indicators: slope and intercept (A0) of the A-S line, and S0, compared early vs late (Fig. 1, p. 1; Sec. 4.1).
- Reliability score per athlete: R = R_c · R_MAE · R_d (Eq. 8, p. 4).

- Text says the "first and second halves" were processed separately (p. 7), but the Fig. 5 caption says first and second 23 minutes of a 45-min segment. Inconsistent.

## Equations     (LaTeX, with variable definitions and units; mark "checked against PDF")
Checked against PDF text (p. 3–4, pdftotext; Fig. 4/5 line equations checked as images, minus signs confirmed).

Neighbourhood indices and central-difference velocity / acceleration ($k$ = frame index, $T$ = frames, $f$ = frame rate Hz, $t_k = k/f$ s, $\tilde p_i$ = smoothed 2D position of athlete $i$ in m, $l_n$ = half-window in frames):
$$k^- = \max(k - l_n, 1), \quad k^+ = \min(k + l_n, T)$$
$$\mathbf v_i(t_k) = \frac{\tilde{\mathbf p}_i(t_{k^+}) - \tilde{\mathbf p}_i(t_{k^-})}{t_{k^+} - t_{k^-}}, \qquad \mathbf a_i(t_k) = \frac{\mathbf v_i(t_{k^+}) - \mathbf v_i(t_{k^-})}{t_{k^+} - t_{k^-}}$$
(velocity m/s, acceleration m/s²; vectors — the paper does not say if A-S uses the magnitude of the vector acceleration or the tangential component.)

Fisher-z averaging of correlations (Eq. 7): $z = \tfrac12 \ln\frac{1+r}{1-r}$, $r_{avg} = \frac{e^{2\bar z}-1}{e^{2\bar z}+1}$.

Reliability (Eq. 8): $R = R_c \cdot R_{MAE} \cdot R_d$, with $R_c$ = Pearson r of predicted vs true speed, $R_{MAE} = 1/(1 + MAE_{speed})$, $R_d = N_{frames}/N_{total\ frames}$.

A-S line: as Morin2021, $a_{max}(v) = A_0 - (A_0/S_0)\,v$.

## Limitations
- Preprint; n = 1 player for the within-match "fatigue" result, no ground truth, no statistics (p. 7–8).
- Ground truth is from video annotations processed with the same method (bias; large l_n may hide high-frequency motion and inflate scores) (p. 8). Authors call for a public GPS benchmark.
- 30-s clips are far too short for a real A-S profile (p. 7). Broadcast tracking loses players off-camera; low-reliability athletes have speed r ≈ 0.49.
- Acceleration from second derivatives is noisy; absolute A0/S0 values "should be interpreted with caution"; only relative changes may be informative (p. 8).
- The fatigue reading of Fig. 5 is weak: implied S0 of 33 m/s in the early fit shows the line is driven by noise. The authors cite Maviel et al. (2024, rugby) recommending ≥ 160 min of match play for a reliable A-S profile (p. 3) — much more than 23 min.
- No context control (score, tactics, role) for the early vs late comparison.

## Use in Running Battery
- Simulator: none directly. Could add realistic tracking noise (speed MAE ~0.2–0.5 m/s, acceleration MAE ~0.3–0.5 m/s² for good tracks) as an optional "broadcast-quality" mode to stress-test the estimator.
- Fatigue estimator: use Morin binning; smooth positions (Kalman) and differentiate over ~1–1.6 s windows; drop speeds > 12–15 m/s. Treat the A-S "capacity check" as low-confidence for windows shorter than a half; ≥ 160 min is the rugby recommendation for a reliable profile. Prefer comparing to a multi-match baseline.
- Agents / claims: may say "a recent preprint explored A-S profiles from broadcast video as fatigue indicators and found it feasible but noisy". Must NOT say "A-S profiles have been shown to decline within a match" — no such evidence here.
- Overlay / UI: shows the topic is current (April 2026); good for the pitch narrative, not for numbers.

## Ideas or questions it raised
- READING_LIST says "fatigue indicators from A-S profiles, computed from broadcast video tracking (SoccerNet)". Roughly correct, but imprecise: the fatigue part is a single qualitative example on one Swiss Super League player; most of the paper is about speed/acceleration accuracy from video.
- Our synthetic data with known truth could answer the question this paper could not: how much data per window is needed before an A-S drop is detectable above noise.
- The vector-acceleration vs forward-acceleration issue matters: changes of direction add "acceleration" that is not forward propulsion. We should use the tangential component (dv/dt of speed magnitude) for A-S.
