# Ju2022: Contextualised peak periods of play in English Premier League matches
Citation: Ju W, Doran D, Hawkins R, Gómez-Díaz A, Martin-Garcia A, Ade JD, Laws A, Evans M, Bradley PS (2022). Contextualised peak periods of play in English Premier League matches. Biology of Sport 39(4):973–983. doi:10.5114/biolsport.2022.112083 (published online 2021-12-30)
Type: match study (tracking data + manual video coding of the tactical purpose of every high-intensity effort)
Context: English Premier League, elite, season 2018–19. 50 matches, 20 teams, 1,265 player observations coded; 583 player observations used for individual analysis (outfield players who played the full match in one position: CDP 179, WDP 147, CMP 167, WOP 54, COP 36); 100 team observations (subs included). Tracking: "cameras situated at roof level" = multi-camera optical tracking; the system name and sampling rate (Hz) are NOT reported (p. 974). Matches with goal difference > 3 or a red card were excluded; team/opponent standard, venue and season phase were balanced (p. 974).
Evidence strength: medium-high. Premier League, 50 matches, rolling-window peaks, validated coding method (agreement ~92% vs gold standard, kappa 0.81–0.94; p. 974). Limits: single coder-based approach, small n for forwards (COP 36, WOP 54), no ball-in-play control, no Hz reported, only one season.

## Definitions and thresholds
- High-intensity running (HIR): speed > 19.8 km/h = > 5.5 m/s, with a minimum dwell time of 1 s (p. 974). Includes sprinting (no separate sprint band).
- Peak period: the 1-, 3- and 5-min window with the most HIR distance, found with a **rolling average** (p. 974–975).
- "Next" period: the 1-, 3- or 5-min window right after the peak (same length). Compared with the **match average** of all windows of that length (stoppage time excluded from the average). Not compared with the peak itself. If the remaining time in the half was shorter than the window, the data point was dropped (p. 975).
- Tactical coding (Table 1, p. 975). One action = one tactical purpose; hybrid actions (70–90% primary / 10–30% secondary) were folded into the primary action; >3 purposes or 50–60/40–50 splits = "Other". About 75% single, 15% hybrid, 10% Other (p. 981).

| Phase | Category | Definition (own words) |
|---|---|---|
| In possession | Push up Pitch | Moves up the pitch to play offside / squeeze a higher line |
| In possession | Break into Box | Enters the opposition box to receive (typically a cross) |
| In possession | Run in Behind / Penetrate | Attacks space behind the defence, overtakes or unbalances it |
| In possession | Over/Underlap | Runs from behind to in front of the ball carrier or receiver |
| In possession | Run with Ball | Moves with the ball (dribbling or running at speed) |
| In possession | Move to Receive / Exploit Space | Moves to get a pass or to create/exploit space (come short, go wide) |
| In possession | Support Play | Supports from behind/level, typically in fast transitions |
| Out of possession | Interception | Cuts out a pass |
| Out of possession | Recovery Run | Runs back toward own goal to get goal-side when out of position |
| Out of possession | Covering | Moves to cover space or a player while staying goal-side |
| Out of possession | Close Down / Press | Runs directly at the ball carrier/receiver, or toward space/players off the ball |
| Unclassifiable | Other | Anything else |

- Effect size scale: trivial ≤ 0.2, small > 0.2–0.6, moderate > 0.6–1.2, large > 1.2–2.0, very large > 2.0 (p. 975). ES type not named (standardised mean difference).

## Equations     (LaTeX, with variable definitions and units; mark "checked against PDF")
None given. Implied definition of the post-peak deficit (our notation, not in the paper):
$$\Delta_{next} = \frac{\overline{HIR}_{next,w} - \overline{HIR}_{match,w}}{\overline{HIR}_{match,w}}\times 100\%$$
where $w \in \{1,3,5\}$ min, $\overline{HIR}_{next,w}$ = HIR distance (m) in the $w$-min window directly after the peak, $\overline{HIR}_{match,w}$ = mean HIR distance (m) over all $w$-min windows of that match (stoppage excluded). Comparison basis checked against PDF p. 975.

## Limitations
- Tracking system and sampling rate not reported; only "roof-level cameras" (optical). Can't check comparability with 10 Hz data.
- No ball-in-play / effective-time control. Authors say the post-peak drop could be fatigue, tactics/pacing or fewer playing opportunities (ball out of play), and they can't separate them (p. 981). Fig. 1 (9 → 2 players involved in the next minute) fits a stoppage after the peak just as well as fatigue.
- Score state and possession share at the time of the peak were not modelled (only extreme scorelines excluded).
- No accelerations/decelerations (p. 981).
- Positions pooled across formations (4-3-3, 3-5-2, …) (p. 982).
- Hybrid actions merged into primary actions.
- Ranges (e.g. 22–44%) mix the three window lengths; exact per-window, per-category values are only in figures.
- ES for category changes are small (0.1–0.3 for players); only team ES reach moderate (up to 0.7).

## Use in Running Battery
- Simulator: (1) Share of HIR by purpose in a peak spell: Recovery Run ~30%, Covering ~23%, Support Play ~11%; use as target mix for peak spells. (2) Post-peak: reduce HIR of out-of-possession categories by ~35–45% (players) for the next minutes vs the player's normal rate, with the biggest drop in the first minute (total −48% at 1 min, −25% at 5 min vs match average). (3) Per-action lengths in peaks: recovery run ~35–42 m, covering ~23–30 m, press ~18–21 m (Table 2). (4) Keep position roles: CDP mostly Covering/Recovery; COP mostly Close Down/Press; WOP Run in Behind/Move to Receive. Allow "tactically required" runs to still happen after a peak (COP Break into Box rebounds at 3 min).
- Fatigue estimator: validation target: after a detected peak, a player's HIR in the next 1/3/5 min should be ~48/30/25% below his own match-average window. Baseline must be the player's match average for the same window length, not the peak.
- Agents / claims: Supported: "After an intense spell, Premier League players do roughly a third to a half less high-intensity covering and recovery running than usual for the next few minutes." Must add: this is a group average and could be tactics or a stoppage, not only fatigue (authors' own caveat). Do not say "20–53%" for individual players: that range is team-level. Individual-player ranges: Covering 22–44%, Recovery Run 34–43%.
- Overlay / UI: post-peak "recovery window" shading for 1–5 min after a detected peak; label categories (recovery run / covering) when the simulator emits them.

## Ideas or questions it raised
- READING_LIST.md check: "50 matches" correct (583 player observations used). "Recovery runs 28–37%": 28–37% is the TEAM range; players 28–34%. "After a peak, players do 20–53% less Covering and Recovery Run": imprecise. 20–53% is the team-level range (Covering 20–41%, Recovery Run 32–53%); players: Covering 22–44%, Recovery Run 34–43%. And the comparison is **next period vs match average**, not vs the peak. PLAN.md's "players do 20–53% less" should say "teams" or use the player ranges.
- The comparison to the match average is the right baseline for a fatigue claim (Fang2024 and Cortez2026 compare to the peak itself, which is mostly selection effect). This makes Ju2022 the best "below normal" number in the group.
- To separate fatigue from context, our simulator could log ball-out-of-play after peaks; the agents should check whether the next minute contained a stoppage before saying "tired".
- Question: does the deficit differ when the peak was out-of-possession vs in-possession? Not analysed.
