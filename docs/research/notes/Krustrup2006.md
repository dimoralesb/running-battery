# Krustrup2006: Muscle and blood metabolites during a soccer game and sprint performance
Citation: Krustrup P, Mohr M, Steensberg A, Bencke J, Kjær M, Bangsbo J (2006). Muscle and blood metabolites during a soccer game: implications for sprint performance. Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise 38(6):1165–1174. doi:10.1249/01.mss.0000222845.89262.cd
Type: match study with physiological sampling (field experiment in friendly games)
Context: 31 Danish **fourth-division** (amateur/semi-pro) male players, age 28 (21–33) y, three friendly games, controlled diet. Group A (n = 11): blood at fixed times, biopsies before/after game, sprint test before and after each half. Group B (n = 20): blood before/after each half and after an intense period in each half; 14 of them biopsied after an intense period in each half; sprint test before the game and after an intense period in each half. Heart rate (Polar, 5-s intervals) for all. Time–motion video for 7 biopsied players only. No positional tracking. (pp.1165–1166)
Evidence strength: medium for the physiology (direct muscle biopsies during match play are rare), low–medium for sprint numbers (small n, lower level, friendlies, test protocol interrupts the game). Intensity was lower than elite (HR 5–10 bpm lower, TD −10%, HIR −20%; p.1171).

## Definitions and thresholds
- Intense period: a period "which included fast running (> 18 km/h = 5.0 m/s) and sprinting (> 25 km/h = 6.9 m/s)" (p.1166); how it was chosen live is not described in detail. HIR in the 5 min before muscle sampling was 145 ± 23 m (p.1171).
- Repeated sprint test: 5 × 30 m, 25 s active recovery (jog back), ~2 min in total; started 20–30 s after the biopsy, so **35–60 s after match play stopped** (p.1166). Infrared gates, 0.01 s precision.
- Biopsies: m. vastus lateralis, 15–30 s after stopping play (p.1166).
- Fibre glycogen: PAS staining, rated full / partly full / almost empty / empty (3 to 0).
- Values: mean ± SEM (HR mean ± SD).

**What does NOT explain temporary fatigue (p.1171–1172)**
| Candidate | Finding |
|---|---|
| Muscle lactate | not correlated with sprint decline (r² = 0.14 1st half, 0.13 2nd half, n.s.; Fig. 6); only ~¼ of the rise seen at exhaustion |
| Muscle pH | not correlated (r² = 0.00 and 0.12) |
| Total muscle glycogen | not correlated (r² = 0.02 and 0.01) |
| ATP, IMP | not correlated (r² ≤ 0.07); ATP only moderately reduced, IMP low → no energy crisis |
| CP | only ~20 mmol/kg d.w. lower; not useful to correlate because CP resynthesises fast (up to 0.5 mmol/kg d.w./s) during the ~15–30 s biopsy delay |
| Blood metabolites | none correlated with sprint decline |
| Blood lactate | not correlated with muscle lactate (r² = 0.25 and 0.06; Fig. 4) → poor marker |
| Open hypothesis | K⁺ accumulation in muscle interstitium (plasma K⁺ ~5 mM, individual > 5.5 mM); "likely multifactorial" (p.1172) |
| End-of-match | sprint decline may be explained by glycogen-empty individual fibres (p.1172) |

**Recovery-timing information**
- Sprint impairment is present 35–60 s to ~3 min after an intense period (test window), so temporary fatigue lasts at least ~1–3 min.
- After the 1st intense period only sprints 3–5 were slower: the effect shows up in **repeated** sprints more than in a single sprint.
- By the end of the 1st half sprint ability was back to baseline (Group A), so recovery occurs within the rest of the half (exact time not reported).
- Blood lactate clearance ~0.25 mmol/L/min vs muscle lactate decrease ~1.8 mmol/L/min (about 7× faster), cited from Bangsbo et al. (p.1173). Muscle lactate, glycogen, ATP recover slowly (< 0.1 mmol/kg d.w./s); H⁺ < 0.1 nmol/L/s (p.1171).

## Limitations
- Fourth-division friendlies, lower intensity than elite; small n (biopsies n = 5–14 per variable).
- The sprint test itself and the biopsy break the game; the "intense period" was chosen by the researchers in real time (selection not fully described).
- Only one muscle (vastus lateralis); fibre-level CP or K⁺ not measured; K⁺ hypothesis untested.
- Sprint times are averages of 5 sprints; % changes small (1.6–3.6%) relative to typical test noise (not reported here).
- No exact recovery time course after intense periods.

## Use in Second Wind
- Simulator: temporary fatigue lowers **repeated-sprint** ability by ~1.5–3.5% in sprint time (first 1–3 min after an intense spell), more in the 2nd half; end-of-match ~3%. Single-sprint top speed in the first effort may be almost unchanged after a 1st-half spell. Recovery to baseline within the remaining minutes of the half. A slow "glycogen" store drained over 90 min (449 → 255 mmol/kg d.w., −42%) is a reasonable hidden state for the end-of-match component.
- Fatigue estimator: do not use lactate-like accumulation models as the explanation for temporary fatigue; a fast-recovering "D′" component is consistent with the data. Speed loss is small (a few %), so a top-speed-based fatigue signal must handle noise.
- Agents / claims: supported: "right after an intense spell, players are a few percent slower in repeated sprints; by the end of the half this has recovered"; "late in the game, about half of muscle fibres are low on glycogen". Not supported: "lactic acid made him tired"; "blood lactate shows how tired he is".
- Overlay / UI: could show "repeat-sprint capacity" rather than "top speed".
