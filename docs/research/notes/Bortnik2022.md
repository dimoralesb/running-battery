# Bortnik2022: Mean and peak physical demands during transitions and high pressing (Polish Ekstraklasa, GPS)
Citation: Bortnik L, Burger J, Rhodes D (2022). The mean and peak physical demands during transitional play and high pressure activities in elite football. Biology of Sport 39(4):1055–1064. DOI printed on the PDF: 10.5114/biolsport.2023.112968 (the "2023" in a 2022 issue looks odd; verify on Crossref). Published online 2022-02-04.
Type: match study (single team, small sample)
Context: one club in the Polish Ekstraklasa (1st division), season 2020–21, Aug–Nov 2020. 10 official matches (9 league + 1 UEFA Champions League qualifier). 23 outfield players who played ≥ 60 min (CB 4, FB 5, CDM 2, CAM 2, CM 2, W 5, FW 3) (p. 1056). Tracking: GPS/GNSS units with inertial sensors (MEMS), Catapult Vector S7, **10 Hz**, worn between the scapulae; inclusion > 6 satellites, HDOP ≤ 1.0 (p. 1057). Transitions coded from video by the club's analysts (Catapult Vision) using the REOFUT framework.
Evidence strength: low-medium. Single team, 10 games, GPS (not comparable 1:1 with optical tracking), team-level results only, no positional breakdown, no possession/opponent context. Useful mainly for the relative ranking of phase types and their duration.

## Definitions and thresholds
- HSRD: > 19.8 km/h (> 5.5 m/s). Sprint distance (SD): > 25.2 km/h (> 7.0 m/s). High-intensity accelerations + decelerations (A+D): > 3 m/s², counted per minute. Acc B3 Dist: distance covered while accelerating > 3 m/s² (p. 1057).
- Phases (p. 1057):
  - **Positive transition (PT)** = counter-attack: starts by winning the ball in play; high-tempo progression exploiting the opponent's imbalance; play more in depth than width; opponent has no time to reorganise.
  - **Negative transition (NT)** = attack-to-defence; defending against an opposition counter-attack (as defined above).
  - **Fast attack (FA)**: starts by winning the ball or from a restart; many penetrative short passes; width and depth; opponent HAS time to reorganise.
  - **High pressure (HP)**: one or more attackers press the opponent near the opponent's box (attacking third) to win the ball and start a counter within the first 3 s of the opponent's possession; defender(s) within 1.5 m of the first attacker.
- Peaks are NOT rolling windows: each transition is an event of ~10 s, and the demand is expressed per minute (m/min) over the transition's own duration. "Transition mean" = average over all transitions; "transition peak" = the highest value in each game, averaged over the 10 games (p. 1057).
- Statistics: univariate ANOVA (games × transitions), partial η²: small 0.01–0.059, moderate 0.06–0.137, large > 0.138 (p. 1057).

Match-to-match CV (Fig. 1B, p. 1058): transitions vs 90-min: TD 6.0% vs 3.3%; A+D 15.4% vs 9.2%; HSRD 17.8% vs 12.3%; SD 26.5% vs 32.3%.

Share of whole-match output that happens during transitions (Fig. 2A, p. 1059): TD 11%, Acc B3 Dist 10%, HSRD 38%, SD 51%. Transitions take roughly 50 × 10 s ≈ 8 min per game, i.e. ~9% of match time (our estimate).

Share of transition sprint distance by type (Fig. 2B, p. 1059): PT 41%, FA 28%, NT 24%, HP 7%.

## Equations     (LaTeX, with variable definitions and units; mark "checked against PDF")
Transition mean and peak (p. 1057, described in words; written by us):
$$\overline{X}_{TA} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N} \frac{x_k}{t_k/60}, \qquad X^{peak}_{TA} = \frac{1}{G}\sum_{g=1}^{G}\max_{k \in g}\frac{x_k}{t_k/60}$$
$x_k$ = distance (m) or count in transition $k$; $t_k$ = its duration (s); $N$ = number of transitions; $G$ = 10 games. Units m/min or n/min. Wording checked against PDF; the exact computation (team vs player level) is not fully specified.

## Limitations
- One team, 23 players, 10 games, autumn 2020; GPS 10 Hz (GNSS speed and especially > 3 m/s² accelerations are not directly comparable with optical tracking).
- Per-minute rates over ~10-s events inflate values relative to 1-min rolling peaks (a 10-s burst at 5 m/s is "300 m/min"). Don't compare 290 m/min here with 1-min MDPs (~190 m/min in Cortez2026).
- Team-level only; no positions; no individual relative thresholds.
- No opponent, score or formation context (authors' limitation, p. 1060–1062).
- No time course after transitions (no post-transition recovery data).
- A+D in transitions is only 145% of match average, which may be low because ~10-s windows capture few discrete events.

## Use in Second Wind
- Simulator: phase types and rates. ~50 transitions per team per match (32–68), ~10 s each (peak 22–27 s); mix PT 28%, NT 31%, FA 26%, HP 16%. During transitions: ~3.4 m/s average speed (TD ~200 m/min), HSRD ~6.6× and sprint ~9× the match average; NT has highest total distance and A+D, PT the most sprinting, HP high A+D but low running. About half of all sprint distance and ~38% of HSR should occur in transitions.
- Fatigue estimator: transition episodes are where large chunks of high-speed load come from; a "transition counter" can help explain battery drops.
- Agents / claims: Supported: "counter-attacks and the moments right after losing the ball are among the most intense phases of play." Precise version: losing the ball (NT) = highest total running and accel/decel rate; winning it (PT) = most sprinting. Must mention it is one Polish team, GPS.
- Overlay / UI: tag transitions on the timeline; "tired moment" candidates = NT episodes for players with a low battery.

## Ideas or questions it raised
- READING_LIST.md check: citation correct (Biol Sport 39(4):1055–1064; DOI missing in READING_LIST; PDF shows 10.5114/biolsport.2023.112968). "Negative transition has the highest running demand" is only true for **total distance per minute** (and A+D, HSRD not significantly above PT). **Positive transitions have the highest sprint demand.** "Transitions are the most demanding moments" — supported vs the 90-min average, but the study never compared transitions with rolling 1-min peaks of the same players. No positional data, despite the brief asking for it.
- This is GPS 10 Hz data — the same sampling rate as our synthetic tracking, but GNSS noise differs from optical.
- Combine with Ju2022 (peak spells mostly recovery runs and support play, i.e. transition actions): peaks and transitions are probably the same moments; our simulator should make most peak spells happen inside transition sequences.
