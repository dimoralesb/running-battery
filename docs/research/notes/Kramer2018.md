# Kramer2018: CS and D′ norms for high-level male rugby players (running 3MT)
Citation: Kramer M, Clark IE, Jamnick N, Strom C, Pettitt RW (2018). Normative data for critical speed and D′ for high-level male rugby players. J Strength Cond Res 32(3):783–789. doi:10.1519/JSC.0000000000001880
Type: cross-sectional, normative (stanine tables).
Context: 34 South African male rugby players recruited, 30 analysed (2 did not finish, 2 excluded for pacing: their last 30 s was faster than 120–150 s). "University or professional rugby athletes", 24 ± 3 y, 81 ± 12 kg. Preseason, 2–3 weeks before matches. Running 3-min all-out test (3MT) on an outdoor track, wrist GPS (Garmin Forerunner 305) **sampling every 3–4 s**, cross-checked against 30 Hz video splits at 20 m cones ("nearly identical") (Procedures).
Evidence strength: **medium for the numbers, low for our use.** Established journal, Crossref checked 2026-10-17 (J Strength Cond Res 32(3):783–789). Rugby, not football; mixed university/professional; coarse GPS; no reliability data (p.787).

## Definitions
- CS = mean speed from 150 to 180 s of the 3MT. D′ = 150 s × (S150 − CS), with S150 the mean speed over the first 150 s (p.784).

## Key numbers
- **All 30: CS 3.87 ± 0.55 m/s (13.9 km/h), D′ 288 ± 49 m** (abstract 288.2 ± 49.1), S150 5.79 ± 0.48 m/s, distance in 180 s 873 ± 71 m (Table 2, p.785; the abstract says 871.5 ± 71.9).
- Forwards (n = 18): CS 3.73 ± 0.51, D′ 283 ± 42 m. Backs (n = 12): CS 4.09 ± 0.57, D′ 295 ± 59 m. No significant difference (CS p = 0.07, D′ p = 0.51) (Table 2; Results, p.786).
- 13 of 30 players had D′ above 300 m; the group had "never observed" that before (Results, p.786).
- Stanines (Table 3, p.786): D′ "average" band about 263–276 m; CS "average" about 3.54–3.64 m/s.
- Comparisons quoted (second-hand): Olympic male distance runners D′ 194 ± 18 m (Dekerle); university female soccer players D′ 213 m (Clark 2013) (p.786).

## Equations
D′ = 150 × (S150 − CS) (p.784). Check: 150 × (5.79 − 3.87) = 288 m.

## Main message
Rugby players have moderate CS and very high D′. The authors expected this: rugby relies more on short sprints and anaerobic capacity than football (p.784, p.786).

## Limitations and errors (read critically)
- **S180 is wrong in Table 2**: 3.81 ± 0.66 m/s, which is below CS and does not fit 873 m in 180 s (4.85 m/s, derived). Probably a different quantity or a typo.
- Group sizes are swapped: Methods say forwards n = 12, backs n = 18 (Statistical analyses); Table 2 says 18 and 12.
- The Discussion uses a total distance of 810 m (288/810 = 33%, p.787); Table 2 says 873 m.
- The female soccer players' D′ is given as 213 m in one paragraph and 188.8 m in another (p.786–787).
- GPS at 3–4 s per sample is coarse for the first 10–20 s of an all-out run, where speed changes fastest.

## Use in Running Battery
- **Simulator / estimator prior**: an upper reference for D′, not a football prior. The rugby mean (288 m) sits above every football value we have (university 173 [Sutcliffe2025]; mixed field-sport athletes 208 [Kramer2020]) and the authors explain it by the sport. So it does **not** argue for raising our D′ from 180 m (PLAN decision 1 kept 2026-10-17). It does confirm the spread: SD about 50 m again.
- **Agents / claims**: none.

## Ideas or questions it raised
- D′ SD ≈ 50 m appears in every group (rugby, university football, field-sport athletes, distance runners), so the between-player spread is better known than the professional mean.
