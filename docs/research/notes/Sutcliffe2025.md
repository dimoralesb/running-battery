# Sutcliffe2025: 3-min all-out running test, CS / D′ and D′ balance on match GPS
Citation: Sutcliffe DP, Kramer M (2025). Exploring the utility of the 3-minute all-out running test in assessing match performance and external loads in university soccer players. PLoS ONE 20(6):e0323655. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0323655. Data: Harvard Dataverse doi:10.7910/DVN/5FTIRQ
Type: match study (small, cross-sectional, correlational)
Context: North-West University (Potchefstroom, South Africa), male university players competing in the ABC Motsepe (regional) League; n = 26 outfield players (21.4 ± 2.6 yr, 62.0 ± 7.5 kg); 3–8 league matches per player, 5 Feb – 3 Mar 2023, two matches per week, 2 × 45 min on grass (p. 3–4). GPS: Catapult MinimaxX S4, 10 Hz, Doppler speed, for both the 3MT and matches (p. 3–4). Efforts counted if speed held ≥ 0.5 s (p. 4).
Evidence strength: low. Small sample, university level, many correlations without correction, descriptive values only shown in a figure, and the D′bal method (τ) is not reported. Some reported statistics are inconsistent (see Limitations).

## Definitions and thresholds
- 3MT: 3 min all-out run around two grass football pitches in boots, no time cues, verbal encouragement; analysed in Matlab following Kramer et al. 2020 (ref 21) (p. 3–4). The paper does not restate the formulas (standard 3MT: CS = mean speed of last 30 s; D′ = distance above CS).
- Speed thresholds (Table 1, p. 5): LSR < 4 m/s (14.4 km/h); HSR > 4 m/s (14.4 km/h); VHSR > 5.5 m/s (19.8 km/h); "Smax" efforts > 90% of 3MT Smax; match Smax efforts > 90% of match max.
- CS-based variables: Time>CS, Initiate_CS, D>CS, D<CS; ΔD′bal / D′bal Diff = drop from max to min D′bal in a match; D′bal End = D′bal at final whistle (Table 1).
- D′bal method: D′ depleted when speed > CS, recovers when speed < CS (Fig. 1 caption, p. 6). Refs given: Kirby et al. 2021 (track running) and Skiba & Clarke 2021 (W′bal review) (p. 3, refs 17–18). The time constant τ and the exact equation used are NOT reported.
- Load formulas (Eqs. 1–2, p. 4–5): Load_traditional = D_LSR + D_HSR + D_VHSR + Initiate_HSR + Initiate_VHSR + Initiate_Smax; Load_modified = D<CS + D_CS + D_Smax + Initiate_CS + Initiate_Smax + ΔD′bal; both divided by 1000. (Adds metres and counts together, which is dimensionally odd.)
- Data normalised per minute played (p. 4) — but Fig. 2 values look like per-match totals.

## Equations     (LaTeX, with variable definitions and units; mark "checked against PDF")
Load equations checked against PDF (p. 4–5, text).
$$Load_{trad} = D_{LSR} + D_{HSR} + D_{VHSR} + N_{HSR} + N_{VHSR} + N_{S_{max}}$$
$$Load_{mod} = D_{<CS} + D_{>CS} + D_{S_{max}} + N_{CS} + N_{S_{max}} + \Delta D'_{bal}$$
$D$ = distances (m) in each band; $N$ = number of efforts initiated above each threshold; $\Delta D'_{bal}$ = largest D′ drop in the match (m); both loads ÷ 1000.
No D′bal equation is given in the paper.

## Limitations
- Authors' own: external load only, university males, few matches in one season, matches only (no training) (p. 10).
- D′bal τ and equation not reported → cannot reproduce the D′bal numbers.
- Values only in a figure with integer-rounded labels; CS "4 (0)" hides the real mean (~3.6 m/s).
- Statistical inconsistencies: abstract says "3MT-derived Smax" correlated with initiating HSR (r = 0.73) and VHSR (r = 0.69, p < 0.05), but Fig. 3 and the text show these r values are for Initiate > 90% 3MT Smax vs Initiate_VHSR and D>VHSR; 3MT Smax itself is not related (r ≈ −0.19 / −0.09). Text reports "VHSR r = 0.33, p = 0.010" (p. 7), which is not possible with n = 26 (r = 0.33 gives p ≈ 0.1); Fig. 3 shows CS vs Initiate_VHSR r = 0.33 without a star.
- Many correlations (17 × 17 matrix), no correction for multiple testing. Several "strong" correlations are mathematical artefacts (D′ vs D′bal End r = 1.00; Time>CS vs D>CS 0.98).
- 3MT Smax (≈ 7.45 m/s) is lower than match Smax (≈ 8.0 m/s), so the 3MT underestimates top speed.
- Sample-size calculation (paired t-test, d = 0.5) does not match a correlation design.

## Use in Running Battery
- Simulator: plausible field-test CS ≈ 3.6 m/s (range ≈ 2.8–4.4) and D′ ≈ 173 ± 54 m (range ≈ 90–325) for amateur/university players. Pro players likely higher CS; Kramer 2020 quasi-norms for field sports ≈ 3.5 m/s and ≈ 225 m (quoted in Liu2021). Target: a typical player spends ~8 min (3–16) above CS per match and the deepest D′bal drop is ~21% (14–35%) of D′.
- Fatigue estimator: the D′bal-from-GPS idea works in practice, but with a lab CS the battery rarely goes below ~65–80% of D′ in a match. A "low battery" alert near 0 would almost never fire; thresholds should be relative (e.g., deepest 10% of the player's own range) or our τ/CS choices must be justified.
- Agents / claims: may say "D′ balance has been applied to match GPS in university football (small study)". Must not say "D′bal predicts match performance" — only correlations, mostly trivial or artefactual.
- Overlay / UI: Fig. 1 (speed trace with CS line and D′bal curve) is the model for our player card.

## Ideas or questions it raised
- READING_LIST says "use this one for the D′bal method details only". Wrong in practice: the paper gives no τ and no D′bal equation (only references to Kirby 2021 and Skiba & Clarke 2021). For method details read Skiba & Clarke 2021 (IJSPP 16:1561–1572) or Kirby 2021 (J Appl Physiol 131:1532–1542) — not in our folder.
- Players end matches with D′bal ≈ 95% of D′ and the deepest dip is ~21%. With τ in the 300–400 s range the battery would stay lower longer; this suggests either short τ or lab CS too high relative to match running. Our synthetic test can show which τ gives realistic dips.
- Raw data are public (Harvard Dataverse) — possibly usable to calibrate D′bal dips (check licence before use).
