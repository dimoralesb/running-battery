# Bradley2013: Running drops in 5-min periods: fatigue, pacing or situation?
Citation: Bradley PS, Noakes TD (2013). Match running performance fluctuations in elite soccer: indicative of fatigue, pacing or situational influences? Journal of Sports Sciences 31(15):1627–1638. DOI: 10.1080/02640414.2013.796062
Type: match study
Context: English FA Premier League, seasons 2006/07–2008/09. Prozone multiple-camera optical tracking (cameras at roof level); sampling rate NOT reported in the paper. Part 1 (5-min periods by first-half activity level): 169 outfield players, single observations, from 186 (17 removed to separate groups); only matches between teams of similar end-of-season rank (top/middle/bottom), equal home/away. Part 2: score line n = 54, match importance n = 55, substitutes n = 65 (Table I, article p. 3). Match selection for importance advised by two UEFA-qualified coaches.
Page numbering: the PDF is the Taylor & Francis advance version with article pages 1–12 (= journal pages 1627–1638) plus a cover page; "p. X" below = article page X (PDF page X + 1).
Evidence strength: medium. Large, elite, optical-tracking sample and an explicit attempt to separate fatigue from pacing and context. But: cross-sectional single observations, 5-min fixed periods (not rolling), small position subsets in Part 2 (n = 7 attackers / wide midfielders), means ± SEM (not SD), and many comparisons with Bonferroni correction. Old data (2006–09): EPL running volumes are higher now (Allen2024).

## Definitions and thresholds
Speed categories (p. 2), converted to m/s:
| Category | km/h | m/s |
|---|---|---|
| Standing | 0–0.6 | 0–0.17 |
| Walking | 0.7–7.1 | 0.19–1.97 |
| Jogging | 7.2–14.3 | 2.0–3.97 |
| Running | 14.4–19.7 | 4.0–5.47 |
| High-speed running | 19.8–25.1 | 5.5–6.97 |
| Sprinting | > 25.1 | > 6.97 |
- High-intensity running (HIR) = running + high-speed running + sprinting = ≥ 14.4 km/h (≥ 4.0 m/s) (p. 3). NOTE: this is a much lower threshold than the "HSD > 19.8 km/h (5.5 m/s)" in Thoseby2022/2023 and Bradley et al. 2014. Bradley2013 HIR values (~28 m/min) are NOT comparable with HSD values (~5–9 m/min).
- All distances expressed as m/min so stoppage-time periods can be compared (p. 2).
- Activity groups (Part 1, p. 3): first-half total distance percentile: low ≤ 30th, moderate 35th–65th, high ≥ 70th.
- Peak 5-min HIR: the most intense pre-defined 5-min period; compared with the average 5-min period (excluding the peak) and the next three 5-min periods (p. 3).
- Score line (p. 3): "competitive" = goal difference ≤ 1 for the whole 90 min; "heavily won/lost" = final difference ≥ 3 goals.
- Match importance (p. 3): "critical" = result directly affected title, European places or relegation, plus local derbies; vs lower-importance matches in the same part of the season.
- Substitutes (p. 3): same players, same time period, as substitute vs when completing a full match. Early sub 45–65 min (n = 40), late 65–90 min (n = 25).
- ES (Cohen): trivial < 0.2, small 0.2–0.6, moderate 0.6–1.2, large 1.2–2.0, very large 2.0–4.0 (p. 4).

### 5-min periods by activity group (p. 4; Fig. 1a–c, p. 5)
- High group: less TD in first 10 min of 2nd half vs same 1st-half period (ES 0.9–1.0) and at 70 and 85 min (ES 0.8–0.9). Less HIR in the first 10 min of 2nd half vs 1st half (P < 0.05, ES 0.6–0.7); not in moderate/low (ES 0.2–0.4).
- All groups: HIR fell from first to last 5-min of the 1st half (P < 0.01, ES 0.8–0.9). In the 2nd half, first vs last 5-min HIR was similar (ES 0.2–0.5); 2nd-half stoppage time similar (ES 0.0–0.3).
- TD: all groups declined first vs last 5-min of 1st half and stoppage (ES 1.0–1.3); in the 2nd half low group ES 0.5–0.8, high group first vs last 5-min only (ES 0.5–0.8), moderate no difference (ES 0.4–0.6).
- Sprinting: no differences in any 5-min comparison (too variable).
- Approx. HIR values (≥ 4.0 m/s), read from Fig. 1b (p. 5):

| Moderate | ~32 | ~25 | ~24.5 | ~28 | ~27 | ~26 | ~27.5 |

- Approx. TD, read from Fig. 1a (p. 5): high ~140 m/min at 0–5 min, ~112–113 at 85–90; low ~110 at 0–5, ~89 at 85–90.

### All players pooled, 5-min (p. 4)
- TD greater in the first 10 min of 1st vs 2nd half (ES 0.5–0.6); 40 vs 85 min (ES 0.4). First vs final 5-min of each half: ES 0.6–1.0; 1st-half stoppage lower than first 5 min (ES 0.2–0.9).
- HIR greater only in the first 10 min of the 1st half vs same period of the 2nd half (ES 0.3–0.4). After that, no 1st-vs-2nd differences; the most similar periods were the stoppage times. HIR first vs final 5-min + stoppage: 1st half ES 0.5–0.6 (P < 0.01); 2nd half ES 0.1–0.3 (n.s.).
- No "end spurt" in 2nd-half stoppage time (p. 10).

### By position, 5-min (p. 4; Fig. 1d–f, p. 5)
- TD, first 10 min of 2nd vs 1st half: CD, CM, WM lower (ES 0.7–0.9); FB lower only in the second 5-min period (ES 0.7).
- TD, first vs last 5-min within halves: FB and ATT both halves (ES 0.8–1.0); CD, CM, WM first half only (ES 1.2–1.4).
- HIR, first 5 min of 2nd vs 1st half: only midfielders lower (P < 0.01, ES 0.7).
- HIR, first vs last 5-min of 1st half: ATT, CM, WM lower (ES 0.8–1.2); not FB and CD (ES 0.4–0.5). FB 1st-half stoppage lower than first 5 min (ES 0.8).
- Approx. HIR at 0–5 min, read from Fig. 1e (p. 5): WM ~44, CM ~40, FB ~34, ATT ~32, CB ~23 m/min. At 85–90 min: WM ~33.5, CM ~29.5, FB ~24, ATT ~20.5, CB ~20 m/min.

### Recovery after the most intense 5-min period (p. 6; Fig. 2, p. 6)
- HIR in the 5 min after the peak was 8% below the match-average 5-min period (ES 0.2; P < 0.01 in text, P < 0.05 in abstract and figure legend), and back to average from the second 5-min period on.
- Approx. values, read from Fig. 2: peak 5-min ~45.5 m/min; post 0–5 min ~24.8; post 5–10 ~25.3; post 10–15 ~27.6; average 5-min (minus peak) ~26.8 m/min (HIR ≥ 4.0 m/s).
- Authors say fixed 5-min periods probably underestimate the dip; Mohr 2003 found −12% with a rolling method (p. 10). Large ball-out-of-play stoppages also cause drops (one match: big stoppage at 20–25 min caused a large drop, no numbers; p. 10).

### Match importance (Table III, p. 8; text p. 6)
- All players (n = 55): TD total 113.1 critical vs 112.1 lower; HIR 28.0 vs 27.3; SPR 2.75 vs 2.60 m/min. No overall difference.

### Mechanisms cited (secondary, from other studies)
- Muscle temperature drops ~2 °C over half-time (Mohr 2004), linked to lower sprint performance at the start of the 2nd half (p. 9).
- Muscle glycogen depletion is associated with the sustained 2nd-half decline (Krustrup 2006; p. 9).

## Limitations
- Fixed 5-min periods, not rolling: underestimates peaks and post-peak dips (authors, p. 10).
- Activity groups are confounded with position: "high" group is mostly midfielders, "low" mostly CD and attackers (p. 9). So "high first half → bigger 2nd-half drop" may be position/role, not fatigue.
- No measure of ball-in-play time, possession, or opponent strength in this paper. The possession and opponent points in the discussion come from other studies (Bradley et al. 2013 possession paper, Lago 2009). The lead researcher's brief asked for "effect of possession": this paper does not measure it.
- Small position subsets in Part 2 (n = 7 for ATT and WM in score analysis); single observations in heavily won/lost and critical matches vs a median of two in the comparison condition.
- Data as mean ± SEM, so the spread between players is much larger than the ± values suggest.
- Distance thresholds only; accelerations not measured (authors note this, p. 11).
- Prozone sampling rate not stated. 2006–09 EPL data; modern EPL is faster.
- Abstract and text disagree on the post-peak P value (P < 0.05 vs P < 0.01).

## Use in Second Wind
- Simulator:
  - 2nd-half decline should depend on first-half load: high-work players lose ~12% HIR in the 2nd half; low-work players lose ~0%.
  - Add a short dip in the first 5–10 min after half-time (cool muscles / tempo), stronger for high-work players and midfielders.
  - Add a post-peak HIR dip of ~8% for ~5 min after the most intense 5-min period (fixed 5-min method; real dip is probably larger and longer, see Leifsson2024/Schimpchen2020).
  - Score effects: when a team leads by ≥ 3, its CDs do ~10–17% less HIR and its attackers ~15% more HIR and ~50% more sprinting; a heavily losing team keeps its 2nd-half HIR.
  - Substitutes: +7–8% TD and +14–16% HIR (≥ 4.0 m/s) vs the same player's full-match level in that period; largest in central midfielders; attackers no clear gain.
  - Ball-out-of-play stoppages should lower running in that period (no numbers here).
- Fatigue estimator: a fall in 5-min sprint distance alone is not a usable fatigue signal (no differences found at any period, too variable). Use HIR/HSD over longer windows and compare to the player's own earlier level, adjusted for first-half load.
- Agents / claims: rules for when NOT to call a drop "fatigue" (these feed the Verifier):
  1. Drop in the first 5–10 min of the 2nd half: possible re-warm-up / tempo effect; compare with the same player's later 2nd-half periods, not with the first 10 min of the match (an inflated benchmark).
  2. Drop in total distance with HIR maintained: likely pacing (sparing low-intensity running), not fatigue.
  3. Team heavily ahead: lower HIR in CDs is tactical (sitting deep). Do not call it fatigue.
  4. Team heavily behind: HIR held; a drop here is more likely fatigue than tactics.
  5. Player had low first-half load: a 2nd-half drop is unlikely to be fatigue.
  6. Drop during a long stoppage or low ball-in-play period: not fatigue.
  7. Post-peak dip: small (−8%, ES 0.2) and only ~5 min; say "catching breath after a big spell", not "exhausted".
  8. Role: attackers and wide midfielders' drops can reflect their role; compare within position.
  Allowed: "his high-intensity running is down after a very busy first half". Not allowed: "he slowed down at the start of the second half because he is tired".
- Overlay / UI: show the score line and time-in-play next to any "tired" badge so fans see the context check.

## Ideas or questions it raised
- READING_LIST.md claim ("Less running is not always fatigue") is correct but very thin. Precisions: (1) data are 5-min periods, not 15-min; (2) HIR here = ≥ 14.4 km/h (4.0 m/s), not ≥ 19.8 km/h; (3) the paper measured score line, match importance and substitution, NOT possession or opponent quality; (4) match importance had no overall effect.
- Our simulator knows the "true" fatigue, so we can test the Verifier: create matches where a CD's HIR drops because his team leads 3–0 (tactical) and check that the agent does not label it fatigue.
- The "high vs low first-half activity" result suggests the estimator should track cumulative first-half load (metabolic power) as a prior for 2nd-half decline.
- Question: is the post-half-time dip in our synthetic matches made by the physiology model or by tempo? We should keep the two separable so the Verifier can tell them apart.
