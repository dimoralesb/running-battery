# Bradley2009: High-intensity running in FA Premier League matches (2005/06)
Citation: Bradley PS, Sheldon W, Wooster B, Olsen P, Boanas P, Krustrup P. High-intensity running in English FA Premier League soccer matches. J Sports Sci. 2009;27(2):159-168. doi:10.1080/02640410802512775
Type: match study (league sample)
Context: English Premier League, 2005/06, 28 matches between teams of similar league position, played at similar times of day. 370 player-match files from players who completed the whole match: CB 92, FB 84, CM 80, WM 52, A 62 (p.160). Optical: ProZone v3.0, 8 colour cameras at roof height, **10 Hz** (p.160). Partly ProZone-authored (2 of 6 authors from ProZone Sports Ltd).
Evidence strength: medium. Large sample for its time, multi-club, but 20 years old, ProZone 10 Hz (values not comparable to TRACAB / Second Spectrum 25 Hz), and only full-match players (selection bias toward fitter players / fewer substitutions).

## Definitions and thresholds
Journal pages (PDF page = journal page - 157; PDF p.1 is a download cover page).
| Category | km/h | m/s |
|---|---|---|
| Standing | 0-0.6 | 0-0.17 |
| Walking | 0.7-7.1 | 0.19-1.97 |
| Jogging | 7.2-14.3 | 2.0-3.97 |
| Running | 14.4-19.7 | 4.0-5.47 |
| High-speed running (HSR) | 19.8-25.1 | 5.5-6.97 |
| Sprinting | > 25.1 | > 6.97 |
| **High-intensity running (HIR)** | **> 14.4** | **> 4.0** (running + HSR + sprint) |
| **Very high-intensity running (VHIR)** | **> 19.8** | **> 5.5** (HSR + sprint) |
(p.160.) **Warning: "high-intensity running" in this paper starts at 14.4 km/h (4.0 m/s), not 19.8 km/h.** The modern "HSR > 5.5 m/s" equals this paper's VHIR.
- Peak HIR: the 5-min window with the most HIR, per player (pre-defined 5-min blocks, not rolling) (p.160).
- Recovery time: time between VHIR bouts (> 19.8 km/h) (p.160).
- Maximal running speed: "the top speed a player reached in 5-min periods" (p.160). The whole-match values in Table I look like the **mean of 5-min peak speeds**, not the single match maximum (see Limitations).
- Reliability (p.161-162): intra-observer CV 0.9-2.4% (sprinting 2.4%); inter-observer CV < 2%, except sprinting 3.5%.

## Limitations
- 2005/06 ProZone 10 Hz. Distances above 5.5 and 7 m/s are not comparable with TRACAB / Second Spectrum (Allen2024 cites ~50% between-system differences in sprint distance).
- "Max running speed" (7.3-7.9 m/s, 26-28.5 km/h) is far below modern season peaks (Kavanagh2024: 9.4-9.8 m/s). The likely reasons are the definition (probably the mean of 5-min peaks), 10 Hz smoothing, and the era. Do **not** use it as a top-speed target.
- Fixed 5-min blocks underestimate the true peak and the true post-peak drop (authors admit this, p.167). Also, "peak followed by drop" is partly regression to the mean: any maximum chosen from noisy blocks is followed by a lower value. The paper does not correct for this.
- Only full-match players; only matches between similar-ranked teams; no score-state control. The authors say the end-game deficit could also be a slower game pace (p.167).
- HIR (> 14.4 km/h) is dominated by the 14.4-19.8 km/h band. The ~20% end-game drop is mostly in that band. HSR and sprint did **not** differ between halves.

## Use in Running Battery
- Simulator: event structure. About 35 sprints per match (> 25.1 km/h), about 75 VHIR bouts, time-above-threshold proportions, VHIR recovery gaps of ~50-100 s by position (WM 51 s, CM 62 s, A 73 s, FB 74 s, CB 101 s). Use it for **shape** (within-half decline, end-game ~20% drop in > 4 m/s running, post-peak drop of ~45% vs peak / ~6% vs mean). Scale absolute levels to modern Second Spectrum targets.
- Fatigue estimator: (1) post-peak 5-min drop (~45% vs peak, 6% below mean) = "transient fatigue" signature; (2) recovery time between VHIR bouts rises from ~65 s to ~83 s (+28%) from first to last 15 min → candidate feature "inter-effort recovery time". (3) Top speed barely changes, so fatigue shows in volume and frequency of efforts, not in top speed. This is a warning for our "low energy lowers top speed" assumption.
- Agents / claims: allowed: "In 2005/06 EPL data (ProZone), running above 14.4 km/h was ~20% lower in the last 15 min than in the first 15 min, for all positions." The verifier must check the threshold: it's > 14.4 km/h, not > 19.8 km/h. Claims that HSR (> 19.8) drops ~20% by the end are **not** supported by this paper's half-by-half data.
- Overlay / UI: "time since last high-speed effort" vs the position norm could be a simple live indicator.

## Ideas or questions it raised
- READING_LIST says "Use for speed thresholds only". The thresholds 19.8 / 25.1 km/h are fine, but its "high-intensity" label means > 14.4 km/h. The caller's brief ("HIR > 19.8, sprint > 25.2?") is imprecise: HIR > 14.4, VHIR > 19.8, sprint > 25.1 km/h.
- PLAN.md (Oct 15-17 milestone) uses "distance per position within range of Bradley 2009" as a simulator check. Total distance per position is OK to use as a loose range (ProZone TD is less system-sensitive). But for HSR / sprint, use modern Second Spectrum values (Kavanagh2024, Morgans2025).
- The simulator could add a rule: after the most intense 5 min, the next 5 min drop to just below the player's average (not to zero). This is a target the hidden fatigue model must reproduce, with a regression-to-the-mean check (simulate a no-fatigue control and see how much "post-peak drop" appears anyway).
- Max speed fell in the last 15 min of the first half but not the second half. This suggests pacing or context, not a simple energy store.
