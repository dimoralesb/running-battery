# Thoseby2022: When in each half the peak 1–10 min running demands happen
Citation: Thoseby B, Govus AD, Clarke AC, Middleton KJ, Dascombe BJ (2022). Temporal distribution of peak running demands relative to match minutes in elite football. Biology of Sport 39(4):985–994. DOI: 10.5114/biolsport.2022.110745
Type: match study
Context: Australian A-League, one professional club (Melbourne City staff author), 3 seasons 2015/16–2017/18 (68 matches: 25 + 15 + 26 per season); 44 outfield players, 413 match files (11 ± 8 per player, range 1–33). Only players who played the full 90 min; goalkeepers excluded; all positions pooled. GPS: STATSports, "18 Hz" units (the companion paper Thoseby2023 calls the same units "18 Hz (10 Hz GNSS)"). Speed filtered with 4th-order Butterworth, 1 Hz cut-off; speeds > 10 m/s (36 km/h) and |accel| > 6 m/s^2 set to zero (< 0.01% of points). (p. 986, Methods)
Evidence strength: medium. Good sample of files and a continuous-time method (rolling windows, not discrete bins). But: one club, one league (A-League, below EPL level), positions pooled, full-match players only (selection bias), and only the timing of the single highest window per half is reported, not how often near-peak spells occur.

## Definitions and thresholds
- Total distance (TD) per minute, m/min.
- High-speed distance (HSD): distance above 19.8 km/h = 5.5 m/s, per minute (m/min).
- Average acceleration (AveAcc, m/s^2): the mean of the absolute values of all accelerations AND decelerations over the window (one combined number; accel and decel are not separated). (p. 986)
- Peak demand: the highest rolling-average value for windows of 1, 2, ..., 10 min, computed separately for each half. Rolling windows step by 0.1 min (example "0–1, 0.1–1.1 min", p. 986).
- Timing: the match minute when the peak window STARTS (commencement), within its half; stoppage time is included, so a start after minute 45 of a half is possible. (p. 987)
- Self-containment: % of cases where the whole shorter peak window lies inside the longer peak window (e.g. peak 1-min HSD inside peak 5-min HSD). Labels: very low < 30%, low 30–40%, moderate 40–50%, high 50–60%, very high > 60%. (p. 987, p. 991)
- Skewness bands: |S| < 0.5 symmetric; 0.5–1 moderately skewed; > 1 highly skewed. Kurtosis: 3 = mesokurtic, < 3 platykurtic (light tails). (p. 987)

Summary statements (p. 990 Results, p. 991 Discussion):
- TD and AveAcc peaks are moderately to highly right-skewed (skew 0.7–1.2 for TD, 0.6–1.0 for AveAcc for most windows): they start early in each half. Median start = 7–17 min (TD) and 6–16 min (AveAcc) into the half.
- HSD peaks are "mostly uniformly distributed" in each half (skew 0.0–0.5, kurtosis 1.7–2.1).
- Violin plots (Fig. 1–3, pp. 987–988): TD and AveAcc densities bulge in the first ~10 min of each half, especially for windows ≥ 3 min; HSD densities are close to flat across the half for all windows (approx., read from Figs. 1–3).

Self-containment (Table 2, p. 990):
| Metric | Range | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| TD | 32–47% (low–moderate) | 1-min inside 2-min: 32%; 1-min inside 10-min: 42%; 4-min inside 10-min: 47% |
| HSD | 10–48% (very low–moderate) | 1-min inside 2-min: 10%; 1-min inside 5-min: 35%; 1-min inside 10-min: 48%; 9-min inside 10-min: 13% |
| AveAcc | 30–51% (low–high) | 1-min inside 2-min: 30%; 2-min inside 10-min: 51% |
Text says "less than 55%" overall (p. 991). Meaning: the peak 1-min HSD burst is usually NOT inside the peak 2-min or 5-min HSD window. Peak bursts of different lengths often happen at different times, so players hit near-peak intensities several times per half.

Context numbers cited by the authors (secondary, not their data):
- Bundesliga ball-in-play: ~66% of time in first 15 min of a half vs ~56% in the final 15 min (ref. 9, Linke et al. 2018; p. 991).
- Discrete 1-min epochs underestimate peak TD by 7–10% and peak HSD by 12–25% vs rolling windows (refs 11, 12, 17; p. 986).

## Equations     (LaTeX, with variable definitions and units; mark "checked against PDF")
Average acceleration over a window (definition in words on p. 986; no formula printed; written here from the text, not checked against a printed equation):
$$\overline{|a|}_{w} = \frac{1}{N_w}\sum_{i=1}^{N_w} |a_i|$$
- $a_i$: instantaneous acceleration or deceleration sample (m/s^2), from the filtered GPS speed trace
- $N_w$: number of samples in window $w$
- $\overline{|a|}_{w}$: AveAcc of the window (m/s^2)

Uniform-distribution reference (my addition, to interpret the HSD result): a uniform distribution on $[0, T]$ has skewness 0, kurtosis 1.8, mean $T/2$ and SD $T/\sqrt{12}$. With $T \approx 47$ min (first-half length incl. stoppage, from Thoseby2023 Table 1), mean ≈ 23.5 min and SD ≈ 13.6 min. The 1-min HSD peak start (22 ± 13 to 22 ± 14 min, kurtosis 1.8–2.1) matches this almost exactly.

## Limitations
- One club, one league, positions pooled (authors say clusters too small for position analysis; p. 992).
- Only full-90 players included: players subbed off (often the tired ones) are removed, which can bias timing.
- Only the timing of THE single peak per half is analysed. It does not say how many near-peak spells there are, or their size.
- Start-time truncation (my point, not raised by the authors): a 10-min window can only start up to ~10 min before the end of the half, so a "uniform" start for long windows would already have a lower mean (≈ (47 − 10)/2 ≈ 18.5 min). HSD 10-min starts (mean 15–16) are still a little earlier than that; TD 10-min starts (mean 10–11, median 7–9) are clearly early. The early-TD conclusion survives; the "uniform HSD" claim is strongest for the 1–5 min windows.
- 2nd-half HSD skew 0.4–0.5 is close to the "moderate skew" cut-off, so the 2nd half is slightly less uniform than the 1st.
- The authors' explanations (positive pacing, coaches' plans to dominate the first 15 min, ball-in-play time) are speculation, not tested.
- GPS (10 Hz GNSS) values for AveAcc depend heavily on filtering; not comparable with optical tracking accelerations.
- Within-half timing only; nothing on the whole-match (0–90) distribution, extra time, or score state.

## Use in Running Battery
- Simulator: place each player's peak short HSD burst (1–5 min) uniformly in time within each half (mean ~22 min, SD ~13–14 min of a ~47–50 min half). Place the "high-tempo" phases that drive TD and accel/decel peaks early in each half: the start of the peak 3–10 min TD window should have median ~7–12 min and IQR roughly 2–25 min. A simple way: raise the team tempo / ball-in-play probability in the first ~15 min of each half, and let HSD bursts come from match events (counters, transitions) at any time. Do not attach one big intense spell per half; self-containment < 55% means several separate near-peak bursts.
- Fatigue estimator: the post-peak flag ("within ~5 min of a peak spell") can fire at any minute for HSD, so the estimator must not assume peaks are early. For TD/accel, an early-half peak followed by a drop is the NORMAL pattern, not a fatigue sign.
- Agents / claims: allowed: "the most intense high-speed bursts can come at any time in a half". Not allowed: "he must be tired, his hardest spell was at the start of the half" (TD and accel peaks are early in nearly everyone).
- Overlay / UI: realism-check chart: distribution of the start minute of each player's peak 1/5/10-min window per half in synthetic matches vs Table 1 medians/IQRs (TD early, HSD flat).

## Ideas or questions it raised
- READING_LIST.md claim ("Peaks of total distance and acceleration come early in each half; peaks of high-speed running can happen at any time (uniform distribution)") is correct, with these precisions: (1) it is about timing WITHIN each half, not across the 90 min; (2) it is the timing of the single peak window, pooled across positions, full-90 players only, one A-League club; (3) "uniform" is best supported for 1–5 min HSD windows; the 2nd half is mildly right-skewed (0.4–0.5); (4) the paper gives no peak magnitudes. Magnitudes are in Thoseby2023.
- Validation test for the simulator: compute skewness/kurtosis of synthetic peak start times; target HSD skew ≈ 0–0.5, kurtosis ≈ 1.8–2.1; TD/AveAcc skew ≈ 0.7–1.2.
- Self-containment is a cheap, unusual realism metric: synthetic data should give 1-min-in-2-min HSD containment near 10% and 1-min-in-10-min near 48%.
- Question: are peak HSD bursts linked to match events (goals, counters)? The authors suggest so but do not test it. Our simulator can create them from events and check the timing comes out uniform.
